## 8. Ecology, Ecosystem and Biogeochemical Cycles

### Basic ecology
- **Ecology** – study of relationships between organisms and their environment (term *oekologie*, **Ernst Haeckel, 1866**). **Autecology** = single species; **Synecology** = communities. Levels: organism → population → community → ecosystem → biome → biosphere. **Eugene Odum** – father of modern ecology; **Ramdeo Misra** – father of Indian ecology.
- **Ecosystem** = living (biotic) + non-living (abiotic) components (term by **A.G. Tansley, 1935**). Structure: producers (autotrophs), consumers (primary = herbivores, secondary = carnivores feeding on herbivores, tertiary = top carnivores), **decomposers** (bacteria, fungi), detritivores (earthworm).
- **Ecotone** = transition zone (mangrove between land and sea); **edge effect** = higher diversity there. **Habitat** = place; **niche** = functional role. **Keystone species** (large effect on community, e.g., tiger/sea otter/fig), **indicator species** (**lichens** show SO₂ pollution), **flagship** (tiger, panda), **umbrella** species.

### Energy flow and food chains
- **Grazing food chain** starts with green plants (e.g., Grass → Grasshopper → Frog → Snake → Hawk; here **frog is the secondary consumer**, snake tertiary). **Detritus food chain** starts with dead matter (dominant in forests).
- **Food web** = interconnected chains. Sun is the ultimate energy source; energy flow is **unidirectional**; nutrients **cycle**.
- **Lindeman's 10 % law**: only ~10 % of energy passes to the next trophic level; the rest lost as heat (respiration). Hence food chains have 4–5 links.
- **GPP** (gross primary production) − respiration = **NPP** (net primary production).
- **Ecological pyramids**: **Pyramid of energy is always upright**; pyramid of number inverted for a tree ecosystem (one tree – many insects) or parasite chain; pyramid of biomass inverted in aquatic ecosystems (small phytoplankton biomass supports larger zooplankton/fish biomass).
- **Biomagnification**: increase of concentration of a non-degradable toxin (DDT, mercury) along trophic levels; top carnivores (birds of prey; eggshell thinning) most affected. **Bioaccumulation** = build-up within one organism.

### Interactions and succession

| Interaction | Effect | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Mutualism | +/+ | **Lichen** (alga + fungus), *Rhizobium*–legume, pollinators |
| Commensalism | +/0 | Orchid on mango tree; barnacles on whale |
| Parasitism | +/− | Tapeworm, *Cuscuta*, mosquito |
| Predation | +/− | Tiger–deer |
| Competition | −/− | Gause's competitive exclusion principle |
| Amensalism | 0/− | Penicillium vs bacteria |

- **Ecological succession**: **primary** (bare rock, no soil; **pioneer = lichens/crustose lichens** on rock, then mosses, grasses, shrubs, trees; hydrosere on water, xerosere on dry rock) vs **secondary** (soil present, after fire/flood; faster). **Climax community** = stable final stage. Pioneer species on sand dunes: grasses; in water: phytoplankton.

### Biogeochemical cycles

| Cycle | Type | Key points |
|---|---|---|
| **Carbon** | Gaseous | Atmosphere ~0.04 % (~420 ppm) CO₂; photosynthesis fixes, respiration/combustion release; **oceans** hold the largest active store; fossil fuels, forests are sinks/sources |
| **Nitrogen** | Gaseous | N₂ = 78 % of air; **fixation** (*Rhizobium*, *Azotobacter*, *Anabaena*, *Nostoc*; lightning) → ammonification → **nitrification** (*Nitrosomonas*: NH₃→NO₂⁻; *Nitrobacter*: NO₂⁻→NO₃⁻) → **denitrification** (*Pseudomonas*) back to N₂ |
| **Phosphorus** | **Sedimentary**, no gas phase | Rocks → soil → plants; guano; cause of eutrophication |
| **Water (hydrological)** | Gaseous | ~97 % oceans, ~2.5 % fresh water (most in glaciers); **transpiration** (plants) + evaporation → evapotranspiration |
| **Sulphur** | Sedimentary/gaseous | SO₂ from volcanoes, burning of coal → acid rain |

- Biomes/ecosystems: forest, grassland, desert (terrestrial); pond/lake/river (freshwater), **estuary**, **mangroves** (**pneumatophores/breathing roots**; Sundarbans, Bhitarkanika), **coral reefs**, wetlands.
- **Atmosphere layers**: troposphere (weather, 0–12 km), **stratosphere (ozone layer, 15–35 km)**, mesosphere, thermosphere (ionosphere), exosphere.

**⚡ Exam tip:** Energy pyramid is always upright; phosphorus has no gaseous phase; lichens = mutualism and indicator of SO₂ and primary-succession pioneers; 10 % law = Lindeman.

## 9. Biodiversity and Conservation

### Biodiversity basics
- Three levels: **genetic, species, ecosystem (ecological) diversity**. Term popularised by **E.O. Wilson** (1985; Walter Rosen coined the shortened form). About 1.5–1.8 million species described.
- **Latitudinal gradient**: diversity highest in tropics, decreases to poles. **Species–area relationship** (Alexander von Humboldt): S = C·Aᶻ; on log–log scale a straight line (z ≈ 0.1–0.2).
- India is one of the **17 megadiverse countries** (2.4 % of world land, ~8 % of species).
- **Endemic** species – found only in one area (e.g., lion-tailed macaque in Western Ghats). **Exotic/alien** species – introduced; invasive: **Lantana, Water hyacinth (Eichhornia), Parthenium (Congress grass), African catfish, Nile perch**.
- **Causes of loss ("Evil Quartet")**: habitat loss and fragmentation (the major cause), over-exploitation, alien species invasions, co-extinctions. Extinct: Dodo, Steller's sea cow, passenger pigeon, thylacine; **Cheetah** declared extinct in India 1952; reintroduced at **Kuno NP (MP) in Sept 2022**.

### Hotspots
- **Norman Myers (1988)** concept; criteria: ≥ 1,500 endemic vascular plants and ≥ 70 % original habitat lost; world has **36** hotspots (Conservation International).
- India's **4 hotspots**: **Himalaya, Indo-Burma, Western Ghats & Sri Lanka, Sundaland (Nicobar Islands)**. (Eastern Ghats and Thar are not hotspots.)

### Protected areas and conservation

| Category | Facts |
|---|---|
| **In-situ** | National parks, wildlife sanctuaries, biosphere reserves, conservation/community reserves, sacred groves, tiger reserves |
| **Ex-situ** | Zoos, botanical gardens, **seed/gene banks** (NBPGR, Delhi), cryopreservation, tissue culture, aquaria |
| **National Parks** | ~106; first **Jim Corbett (1936, as Hailey NP; Uttarakhand)** |
| **Wildlife Sanctuaries** | ~560; human activity limited |

- Other projects: **Project Elephant (1992)**, Project Crocodile (1975), Snow Leopard Project, **Project Dolphin (2020)**.
- Famous parks: **Gir** (Asiatic lion, Gujarat), **Kaziranga** (one-horned rhino, Assam), **Keoladeo** (birds, Bharatpur), **Sundarbans** (Royal Bengal tiger, mangroves), Periyar (elephant), **Silent Valley** (lion-tailed macaque, Kerala), **Dachigam** (hangul), **Hemis** (snow leopard, largest NP), Eravikulam (Nilgiri tahr), Bhitarkanika (saltwater crocodile), Namdapha, Desert NP (Great Indian Bustard), Great Himalayan NP.
- **IUCN** (founded 1948; HQ Gland, Switzerland) maintains the **Red List / Red Data Book**. Categories: **EX** Extinct, **EW** Extinct in the Wild, **CR** Critically Endangered, **EN** Endangered, **VU** Vulnerable, **NT** Near Threatened, **LC** Least Concern, DD Data Deficient, NE Not Evaluated. Red pages = threatened; green pages = species recovered.
- **Ramsar sites** (wetlands of international importance): India has about 90 (first: **Chilika** and **Keoladeo**, 1981); **Loktak** on the Montreux Record; **Kanwar (Kabartal)** is Bihar's first.

**⚡ Exam tip:** 4 hotspots = Himalaya, Indo-Burma, Western Ghats–Sri Lanka, Sundaland; first biosphere reserve = Nilgiri 1986; IUCN = Red List; ex-situ = zoo/seed bank; Evil Quartet = habitat loss is number one.

## 10. Pollution, Climate Change, Energy, Waste and Disasters

### Air pollution
- **Primary pollutants**: CO, SO₂, NOₓ, particulate matter, lead, hydrocarbons; **secondary**: ozone (ground-level), **PAN**, sulphuric/nitric acid.
- **PM2.5** = particles ≤ 2.5 µm (reach alveoli; most harmful); PM10 ≤ 10 µm. **CO** binds haemoglobin (carboxyhaemoglobin) ~200× stronger than O₂ → suffocation. **Lead** – nervous-system damage; **SO₂** – respiratory problems; **NO₂** – lung irritation.
- **Smog**: **classical (London)** – smoke + fog + SO₂, reducing, cold humid; **photochemical (Los Angeles)** – NOₓ + hydrocarbons + sunlight → **PAN and ozone**, oxidising, hot sunny.
- **Acid rain**: pH < 5.6; due to SO₂ and NOₓ forming H₂SO₄ and HNO₃; damages marble (Taj Mahal – "marble cancer"), forests, lakes.
- **AQI (India, 2014)**: Good 0–50; Satisfactory 51–100; Moderate 101–200; Poor 201–300; Very Poor 301–400; Severe 401–500.
- Controls: **Electrostatic precipitator** (particulates), scrubbers (SO₂), **catalytic converter** (Pt, Pd, Rh; CO→CO₂, NOₓ→N₂), CNG, BS-VI norms (from April 2020).
- **Bhopal gas tragedy** (2–3 Dec 1984): **methyl isocyanate (MIC)** leak, Union Carbide plant.

### Water, soil and noise pollution
- **BOD** (Biochemical Oxygen Demand) – oxygen consumed by microbes to decompose organic matter; **higher BOD = more polluted**. DO (dissolved oxygen) low → fish die. **Eutrophication**: excess **nitrates/phosphates** (fertilizers, detergents) → algal bloom → oxygen depletion.
- Diseases: **Minamata** (mercury, Japan), **Itai-itai** (cadmium), **blue baby** (nitrate), fluorosis (fluoride), **arsenic** in Ganga plain groundwater (Bhojpur, Buxar, Bhagalpur in Bihar). Thermal pollution (power plants) lowers DO.
- Soil pollution: excess fertilizers/pesticides, salinity, waterlogging, plastics. **Noise**: measured in **decibels (dB)**; > 85 dB harmful; CPCB limits (day/night): residential 55/45, industrial 75/70, silence zone 50/40. Radioactive: Chernobyl 1986, Fukushima 2011.

### Ozone and greenhouse effect
- **Ozone** layer in stratosphere absorbs **UV-B/UV-C**; depleted by **CFCs** (chlorine radicals: one Cl atom destroys ~100,000 O₃); ozone **hole** over **Antarctica** (Sept–Oct); measured in **Dobson units**. UV-B causes skin cancer, cataract. **Vienna Convention 1985**, **Montreal Protocol 1987** (phase-out of ODS), **Kigali Amendment 2016** (HFCs).
- **Greenhouse gases**: CO₂ (largest contributor), CH₄ (rice paddies, cattle, landfills, wetlands), N₂O (fertilizers), CFCs, HFCs, SF₆, water vapour. **GWP (100 yr)**: CO₂ = 1; CH₄ ≈ 28; N₂O ≈ 265–298; SF₆ ≈ 23,500 (highest). Not a greenhouse gas: nitrogen, oxygen, argon.
- Effects: global warming (~1.1–1.2 °C above pre-industrial), **sea-level rise** (thermal expansion + melting land ice), melting glaciers, shifting monsoon, **El Niño** (warm Pacific; weaker Indian monsoon). **Forests are carbon sinks**. Carbon credit = 1 tonne CO₂ equivalent.
- **IPCC** (1988, WMO + UNEP; Nobel Peace 2007) assesses climate science.

### Energy and waste
- **Renewable**: solar, wind, hydro, biomass, geothermal, tidal, ocean, **biogas** (mainly **methane 50–70 %**, from cattle dung; methanogens), green hydrogen. **Non-renewable**: coal, petroleum, natural gas, nuclear fuel (uranium). Coal ranking: peat < lignite < bituminous < **anthracite** (highest carbon). **Solar PV cell** converts **solar (light) energy → electrical energy** (silicon; photovoltaic effect). International Solar Alliance (2015; HQ Gurugram). India's targets: **500 GW non-fossil capacity by 2030; net-zero by 2070** (Glasgow 2021). **MNRE** is the nodal ministry.
- Waste: **3 R's – Reduce, Reuse, Recycle**; segregate wet (biodegradable) vs dry. **Non-biodegradable**: plastics, glass, thermocol. **Vermicomposting** (*Eisenia fetida*). E-waste, plastic, bio-medical (colour-coded bags: yellow, red, white, blue), municipal solid waste rules (2016). **Single-use plastic** ban from 1 July 2022. Extended Producer Responsibility.

### Disaster management
- **Disaster Management Act 2005**: **NDMA** (chair: **Prime Minister**), **SDMA** (chair: Chief Minister; Bihar State Disaster Management Authority), **DDMA** (District Magistrate); NDRF (2006); NIDM. Cycle: mitigation → preparedness → response → recovery.
- **Earthquake**: focus (inside) vs **epicentre** (surface point above); **Richter scale** (magnitude, logarithmic), **Mercalli** (intensity), seismograph. Bihar's north lies in seismic zones IV–V; **Bihar–Nepal earthquake 15 Jan 1934**. Bhuj 2001, Nepal 2015.
- **Floods**: Bihar (north Bihar – Kosi, Gandak, Bagmati, Kamla) is **India's most flood-prone state** (~73 % of area); **droughts** in south Bihar. **Cyclone**: Bay of Bengal (IMD forecasts). **Tsunami** (Japanese "harbour wave"): undersea earthquake; 26 Dec 2004 Indian Ocean; warning centre INCOIS Hyderabad. Lightning also causes many deaths in Bihar.
- **Sendai Framework 2015–2030** (Hyogo Framework 2005–15 preceded it). 13 Oct = International Day for Disaster Risk Reduction.

**⚡ Exam tip:** BOD high = polluted; photochemical smog = PAN + ozone; GWP: SF₆ > N₂O > CH₄ > CO₂; NDMA is chaired by PM; PM2.5 ≤ 2.5 µm; MIC caused Bhopal.

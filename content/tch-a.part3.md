
## 7. Guidance, Counselling, Classroom Management & Mental Health

### 7.1 Guidance

- **Guidance** = process of helping an individual to understand himself and his environment and solve problems. **Frank Parsons** (USA, 1908, Vocational Bureau of Boston; book *Choosing a Vocation*) is the **father of the guidance movement**. In India: **Central Institute of Educational and Vocational Guidance (CIEVG)** under NCERT.
- Types: **Educational** (courses, study habits), **Vocational** (career choice), **Personal/social** (adjustment, emotional), **Health**, **Moral**.
- Principles: for all students (not only problem cases), continuous, based on individual differences, cooperative (teacher, parent, counsellor), the individual makes the final choice.
- Services: orientation, information, **individual inventory** (cumulative record, tests), counselling, placement, follow-up.
- Guidance is broader than counselling; counselling is the **heart** of the guidance programme.

### 7.2 Counselling

| Type | Developer | Features |
|---|---|---|
| **Directive** (prescriptive, counsellor-centred) | **E.G. Williamson** (Minnesota) | Counsellor is active; diagnoses, advises; "the counsellor plays the main role" |
| **Non-directive** (client-centred) | **Carl Rogers** | Client leads, counsellor reflects, accepts, empathy, unconditional positive regard; client finds own solution |
| **Eclectic** | **F.C. Thorne** | Combination, flexible |

- Qualities of a good counsellor: **empathy**, warmth, genuineness, confidentiality, patience, good listening, non-judgemental attitude. The most important quality is usually **empathy/rapport** (trust). Counselling steps: rapport building → exploration → understanding → action/decision → follow-up.
- Group counselling, peer counselling, crisis counselling. A **psychologist** vs **psychiatrist** (medically qualified; prescribes).
- Teacher's role as a first-line counsellor: observe, refer, maintain confidentiality, use cumulative records.

### 7.3 Classroom Management & Discipline

- **Classroom management**: creating and maintaining an orderly, productive learning environment. Discipline = self-control, regulated behaviour for learning.
- **Kounin** (research on classroom management): **withitness** (the teacher knows what is happening in all parts of the room, "eyes in the back of the head"), **overlapping** (handling two things simultaneously), **ripple effect** (correcting one student influences others), **momentum**, **smoothness**, **group alerting**, accountability.
- Approaches to discipline:

| Approach | Feature |
|---|---|
| **Preventive** (best) | Interesting lessons, clear rules, planning, engagement → problems do not arise |
| **Positive/democratic** | Rules made with students, self-discipline, reinforce good behaviour |
| **Punitive/autocratic** | Fear, punishment, suppression — least effective; harmful |
| **Laissez-faire** (permissive) | No control, chaos |

- Other models: **Skinner** (behaviour modification, reinforcement), **Canter** (assertive discipline), **Glasser** (reality therapy, choice theory), **Rudolf Dreikurs** (mistaken goals: attention, power, revenge, inadequacy), **Gordon** (Teacher Effectiveness Training, I-messages), **Jacob Kounin**. 
- **Leadership styles** (Lewin, Lippitt and White): **autocratic**, **democratic** (usually best climate), **laissez-faire**. **Baumrind's parenting styles**: authoritative (best outcomes), authoritarian, permissive (indulgent); Maccoby and Martin added neglectful.
- A student whispers during the lesson: best first step = non-verbal cue (eye contact, move near) or quiet reminder, not public humiliation. Corporal punishment is prohibited (RTE Sec 17; Juvenile Justice Act).
- Good practice: arrive on time, plan lessons, knowing students by name, consistent rules, praise in public/reprimand in private, seating, routines, responding to causes rather than symptoms.
- **Praise**: effective when specific, immediate, sincere. Reward + natural consequences > punishment. **Time-out**, behaviour contract, token economy are applications of operant conditioning.

### 7.4 Mental Health, Adjustment, Defence Mechanisms

- **Mental health** (WHO): state of well-being in which the individual realises his abilities, copes with normal stresses, works productively and contributes to the community. Not merely absence of mental illness. WHO World Mental Health Day: **10 October**. Teachers' role: warm climate, avoid fear/over-pressure, recreation, guidance, early identification.
- **Adjustment**: harmonious relationship between the person and environment; **maladjustment** → frustration, anxiety.
- **Frustration**: blocking of goal-directed behaviour. **Conflict** (**Lewin**): **approach-approach** (two attractive goals; easiest), **avoidance-avoidance** (two unpleasant; "between devil and deep sea"), **approach-avoidance** (one goal with both good and bad aspects; hardest).
- **Stress** (Hans Selye: General Adaptation Syndrome: alarm → resistance → exhaustion). **Anxiety**, depression, phobia, OCD, schizophrenia (psychosis) vs **neurosis**.

**Defence mechanisms (Freud / Anna Freud)** — unconscious ego strategies to reduce anxiety

| Mechanism | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Repression** | Pushing painful thoughts into unconscious | Forgetting a traumatic event |
| **Denial** | Refusing to accept reality | "I did not fail" |
| **Projection** | Attributing own faults to others | Cheating student blames others |
| **Rationalisation** | Giving false but acceptable reasons | "Sour grapes"; blaming pen |
| **Regression** | Reverting to childish behaviour | Teen sucking thumb after failing |
| **Displacement** | Shifting emotion to a safer target | Scolded by teacher, kicks the dog |
| **Sublimation** | Channelling urges into socially approved activity | Aggression into sport/art (healthiest) |
| **Compensation** | Making up weakness in one area by excelling in another | Weak in studies, shines in sports |
| **Reaction formation** | Behaving opposite to real feeling | Excessive politeness to the disliked |
| **Identification** | Adopting traits of admired person | Imitating hero |
| **Intellectualisation** | Detached, logical thinking about emotional matter | |

**⚡ Exam tip:** Frank Parsons = father of guidance; directive = Williamson (counsellor active), non-directive = Rogers (client-centred); eclectic = Thorne. Kounin = withitness. Best discipline = preventive/positive. Sublimation = best defence mechanism; sour-grapes = rationalisation.

## 9. Western Philosophies & Educators

### 9.1 Schools of Philosophy and Education

| School | Representatives | Reality / Aim | Method & role of teacher |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Idealism** | Socrates, **Plato**, Kant, Hegel, Berkeley, Froebel | Mind/spirit is real; aim = **self-realisation**, spiritual and moral development; "ideas are eternal" | Teacher is central (a model, guide); Socratic dialogue, lecture, discussion, **inductive/deductive**; Indian idealists: Vivekananda, Aurobindo, Tagore, Radhakrishnan |
| **Naturalism** | **Rousseau**, Herbert Spencer, Pestalozzi (partly), Tagore (partly) | Nature is reality; **follow nature**; child-centred; freedom, play, no moral instruction imposed; aim = **natural development/self-preservation** | Teacher is an observer/arranger of environment; **learning by experience, play**; "Back to Nature" |
| **Pragmatism** | **Dewey**, William James, C.S. Peirce, **Kilpatrick** (project method) | Truth is what works; experience; social efficiency; **aim = continuous reconstruction of experience** | **Learning by doing**, project, problem-solving, democratic classroom; teacher = guide/friend; "School is a miniature society" |
| **Realism** | **Aristotle**, Comenius, Bacon, Locke | World exists independently; science; aim = knowledge of real world | Sense training, scientific, textbook, objective |
| **Existentialism** | Kierkegaard, Sartre | Individual freedom, choice, responsibility | Dialogue, self-expression |
| **Perennialism / Essentialism / Progressivism** | Hutchins, Bagley / Dewey | Timeless truths / essentials / child's interests | |

- **Idealism → aim: self-realisation**; **naturalism → follow nature**; **pragmatism → experience and utility**; **realism → science**.

### 9.2 Major Educators

- **Jean-Jacques Rousseau (1712-78)**: *Emile* (1762; "Everything is good as it comes from the hands of the Author of nature; everything degenerates in the hands of man"). **Negative education** (shield the child from society's evils; no formal moral lessons until adolescence), "**follow nature**", child-centred, freedom, 4 stages (infancy to 5: physical; 5-12: senses; 12-15: reasoning; 15-20: morals). *Social Contract*. The meaning of "follow nature" = regulate education according to the child's nature (not teacher's whim).
- **John Dewey (1859-1952)**: *My Pedagogic Creed*, *The School and Society*, **Democracy and Education (1916)**, *How We Think*, *Experience and Education*. **Laboratory (Experimental) School, Chicago (1896)**. "**Education is life itself, not a preparation for life.**" "Education is a continuous reconstruction of experience." **Learning by doing**; progressive education; the school as a social institution; **problem-solving method** (steps: felt difficulty, defining, hypothesis, testing). Dewey advocated the project/assignment approach and democratic ideals; his pupil **W.H. Kilpatrick** developed the **Project Method** (1918).
- **Friedrich Froebel (1782-1852)**: German; founder of **Kindergarten** ("child garden", 1837); **play-way**; **gifts** (balls, cubes, cylinders) and **occupations**; idealist; "self-activity", the teacher is a gardener; book: *The Education of Man*. 
- **Maria Montessori (1870-1952)**: Italian doctor; **Casa dei Bambini (Children's House, Rome, 1907)**; **prepared environment**, **didactic (self-correcting) materials**, **auto-education**, freedom within limits, **sensitive periods**, "**absorbent mind**" (0-6), teacher = directress/observer; **no rewards or punishment**; sensory training, practical life exercises. Book: *The Montessori Method*, *The Absorbent Mind*.
- **Pestalozzi**: *object lessons*, "learning by head, heart and hand" (the 3 H's also used by him), love in education. **Herbart**: **five formal steps** (preparation, presentation, association, generalisation, application); psychology-based pedagogy. **Comenius**: *Orbis Pictus* (first picture book), "teach all to all". **Socrates**: dialectic/question-answer, "know thyself". **Plato**: Academy; *Republic*. **Aristotle**: Lyceum, inductive method.
- Other methods: **Dalton plan** (Helen Parkhurst; contract/assignment), **Project method** (Kilpatrick), **Heuristic method** (H.E. Armstrong; discovery), **Kindergarten** (Froebel), **Montessori**, **Play-way** (Caldwell Cook), **Winnetka plan** (Washburne).

**⚡ Exam tip:** Rousseau's *Emile* = naturalism, "follow nature", negative education. Dewey = pragmatism, "learning by doing", "education is life itself", Laboratory School. Froebel = Kindergarten. Montessori = Casa dei Bambini, didactic apparatus, prepared environment. Idealism = self-realisation.

## 10. Education in India: Constitution, RTE, Commissions & Policies

### 10.1 Constitutional Provisions on Education

| Article | Provision |
|---|---|
| **Art. 21A** | Right to free and compulsory education for children **6-14 years** as Fundamental Right (**86th Amendment, 2002**; effective **1 April 2010** with RTE Act) |
| **Art. 45** (DPSP) | Originally free compulsory education to age 14; after 86th Amendment: **early childhood care and education for children below 6 years** |
| **Art. 51A(k)** (Fundamental Duty) | Parent/guardian must provide opportunities for education to child **6-14** (added by 86th Amendment) |
| Art. 28 | No compulsory religious instruction in state-funded institutions |
| **Art. 29** | Protection of interests of minorities (language, script, culture) |
| **Art. 30** | Minorities may establish and administer educational institutions |
| Art. 15(4), 15(5) | Special provisions for SC/ST/OBC (15(5): **93rd Amendment, 2005**, reservation in admission, including private unaided, except minority institutions) |
| Art. 46 | Promote educational interests of weaker sections (SC/ST) |
| Art. 350A | Facilities for **instruction in mother tongue** at primary stage |
| Art. 351 | Promotion of Hindi |

- Education was a **State subject**; transferred to the **Concurrent List** by the **42nd Amendment (1976)** (Entry 25, List III).
- Union list: Central universities, UGC, coordinating higher standards (Entry 63-66).

### 10.2 Right to Education Act, 2009 (RTE)

- Full title: **Right of Children to Free and Compulsory Education Act, 2009**. Passed Aug 2009; **came into force on 1 April 2010** (India became one of 135 countries with such a law).
- Covers children **6-14 years** (classes 1-8, elementary education). Free = no fee/charges (also textbooks, uniforms by govt as per rules); compulsory = govt must ensure admission, attendance and completion.
- **Section 12(1)(c): 25% reservation** at entry level (class I / pre-primary) in **private unaided and special-category schools** for children from **economically weaker sections and disadvantaged groups**; govt reimburses (per-child expenditure).
- **Neighbourhood school**: within **1 km** (classes 1-5) and **3 km** (classes 6-8) (model rules).
- **Pupil-teacher ratio (PTR)**: **30:1** (primary), **35:1** (upper primary).
- **Prohibitions**: capitation fees, **screening procedures** for admission, **corporal punishment and mental harassment** (Sec 17), expulsion/holding back (**no detention** until completing elementary education; amended in 2019 to allow states to detain in class 5 and 8 after regular exam and remedial teaching), private tuition by teachers, running unrecognised schools, non-educational duties for teachers (except census, elections, disaster).
- **School Management Committee (SMC)**: elected; **at least 75% parents/guardians**; prepares school development plan; **50% women** members.
- **Teachers' qualification**: minimum qualification by **NCTE**; **TET** (Teacher Eligibility Test) mandatory (CTET/STET).
- Working days: **200** (classes 1-5; 800 instructional hours/year) and **220** (classes 6-8; 1000 hours); teacher **45 hours/week**.
- **No board examination** till class 8; **Continuous and Comprehensive Evaluation (CCE)**; admission of out-of-school child in **age-appropriate class** with special training (Sec 4).
- Monitoring: **NCPCR** (National Commission for Protection of Child Rights) and SCPCR; grievance redressal by local authority.
- Duties of **appropriate government**: Centre and State; **local authority** for admission/records.
- Constitutional base: **Article 21A**.

### 10.4 NPE 1986 (National Policy on Education; PM Rajiv Gandhi; Programme of Action 1986, revised 1992)

- **National System of Education**; common educational structure (10+2+3), **national core curriculum**; equality of access; **child-centred approach**.
- **Operation Blackboard** (1987; minimum facilities in primary schools: two rooms, two teachers, teaching-learning aids).
- **Navodaya Vidyalayas (JNV)**: residential schools for rural talented children (pace-setting; first 1985-86); **IGNOU** (1985, open university); **Minimum Levels of Learning (MLL)**; **Non-formal education**; **District Institutes of Education and Training (DIET)**; **National Institute of Open Schooling**; **Rural University** model; **Vocational education** (10% by 1990 and 25% by 1995 of higher secondary students); **Special focus on SC/ST, women's equality (empowerment), minorities and the handicapped (integration)**; **6% GDP** target; **early childhood care and education (ECCE)**.
- Programmes: **DPEP** (1994), **SSA** (2001-02), **Mid-Day Meal** (1995), **RMSA** (2009), **Samagra Shiksha** (2018; merged SSA, RMSA, TE).

### 10.5 NEP 2020 (cross-reference)

- Based on the **Kasturirangan Committee**; approved by Union Cabinet **29 July 2020**; replaces NPE 1986.
- **5+3+3+4** structure (replacing 10+2): **Foundational (age 3-8; 3 years preschool + grades 1-2)**, **Preparatory (8-11; grades 3-5)**, **Middle (11-14; grades 6-8)**, **Secondary (14-18; grades 9-12)**.
- **Mother tongue / home language as medium** at least till grade 5 (preferably 8); three-language formula with flexibility; no language imposed.
- **Universal access by 2030**; **GER in higher education 50% by 2035**; **6% of GDP** public expenditure on education.
- Board exams made lower-stakes (can be taken twice a year); **PARAKH** (assessment centre); **no rigid separation of arts/science/commerce**; **vocational education from class 6** with internships; coding from class 6; 360-degree holistic progress card.
- Higher education: **HECI** (single regulator), **NTA**, **multiple entry-exit** (certificate 1 yr, diploma 2 yrs, degree 3/4 yrs), **4-year UG**, **Academic Bank of Credit**, **NRF**, **Multidisciplinary universities (MERU)**.
- Teachers: **4-year integrated B.Ed (ITEP)**; **NCTE**; **NCFTE**; **NPST**. **NIPUN Bharat** (foundational literacy and numeracy; 2021). **NETF** for technology.
- Name of Ministry changed to **Ministry of Education**.

**⚡ Exam tip:** Art 21A (86th Amendment 2002) → RTE Act 2009 (in force 1 Apr 2010) for ages 6-14; 25% reservation in private schools (Sec 12(1)(c)); PTR 30:1 and 35:1; SMC with 75% parents. Kothari = 10+2+3, 6% GDP; Mudaliar = secondary; Radhakrishnan = university; Sargent = 1944 universal elementary. NEP 2020: 5+3+3+4.

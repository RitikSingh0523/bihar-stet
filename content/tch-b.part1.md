> Part II (Teaching Aptitude / Art of Teaching) carries roughly 25-30 of the 150 questions in the paper (about 20%). Expect direct-fact MCQs: who propounded a method, order of Herbartian/5E/microteaching steps, definitions (formative vs summative, validity vs reliability, curriculum vs syllabus, hidden curriculum), item-analysis arithmetic, ICT portals (SWAYAM, DIKSHA...), NEP 2020 numbers and regulatory bodies. ## 1. Teaching-Learning Process

### Meaning and nature
- **Teaching**: an interactive process whose aim is to bring about learning. **Learning**: relatively permanent change in behaviour through experience/practice (not due to maturation, fatigue, drugs).
- **John Adams**: teaching is a **bipolar** process - teacher and learner (face-to-face). **Dewey**: **tripolar** - teacher, child, society (social environment). Later view: tri-polar = teacher, learner, curriculum/content.
- Teaching is a **triadic** activity: input (objectives, content) → process (methods, aids) → output (learning outcome, evaluation).
- Modern view: teacher is a **facilitator / guide / co-learner**; learner is active (constructivism). Effective teaching = **teacher active AND students active**.
- Teaching vs instruction vs training vs indoctrination: instruction = knowledge/skill; training = skill/habit; indoctrination = one-sided belief imposition (not education).

### Phases of teaching (Jackson)

| Phase | Work |
|---|---|
| Pre-active (planning) | objectives, content, method, aids, lesson plan |
| Interactive (execution) | classroom delivery, questioning, reinforcement |
| Post-active (evaluation) | testing, feedback, remedial work |

### Levels of teaching (Morris L. Biggie / Hunt)

| Level | Propounder | Nature | Teacher's role | Learner's role | Evaluation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Memory (thoughtless) | **Herbart** | lowest; rote, cramming of facts | authoritarian, central | passive receiver | recall (essay/short) |
| Understanding | **Morrison** | insight into principles, rules, relationships, "why" | guide | active | understanding-type tests |
| Reflective | **Hunt** (Hunt level) | **highest**; problem-centred, critical thinking, creativity, hypothesis testing | democratic facilitator | highly active, self-driven | problem/creative tasks |

- Memory level = "Herbartian"; Understanding = "Morrisonian"; Reflective = "Hunt".
- Memory level gives knowledge; understanding gives meaning; reflective gives independent thinking. Reflective level includes the previous two.

### Principles and maxims of teaching
- **Principles**: activity/learning by doing; motivation and interest; readiness; individual differences; planning; creativity; feedback and reinforcement; flexibility; democratic atmosphere; correlation with life; revision and repetition.
- **Rousseau - "Follow Nature"**: education must be regulated according to the nature (age, interests, capacities) of the child; teacher is not to impose.
- **Dewey**: "Education is life itself" / learning by doing; **Froebel**: Kindergarten, self-activity; **Pestalozzi**: sense perception; **Montessori**: didactic apparatus, prepared environment.

**Maxims of teaching (Herbert Spencer and others)**

| Maxim | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Known → unknown | start from previous knowledge |
| Simple → complex | easy parts first |
| Concrete → abstract | objects/models first, then ideas (Pestalozzi) |
| Particular → general | examples → rule (inductive) |
| Whole → part | overall view, then details (Gestalt) |
| Analysis → synthesis | break up, then combine |
| Induction → deduction | cases → law; law → cases |
| Psychological → logical | child's interest order before subject-logic order |
| Empirical → rational | experience before reasoning |
| Actual → representative | real thing before model/picture |
| Indefinite → definite | vague idea → exact concept |
| Easy → difficult; near → far | gradation of difficulty / distance |

**⚡ Exam tip:** Memory = Herbart (lowest), Understanding = Morrison, Reflective = Hunt (highest). Adams = bipolar; Dewey = tripolar. "Concrete to abstract" = show real objects/models before definitions.

## 2. Methods of Teaching

### Teacher-centred vs learner-centred

| Teacher-centred | Learner-centred |
|---|---|
| lecture, demonstration, recitation, textbook method | project, heuristic, problem-solving, discussion, role play, cooperative learning, flipped classroom |

### Lecture method
- Oral presentation of content by the teacher; **one-way** communication; economical, covers more content in less time, good for large groups, introduction/summary.
- **Weakness**: learner passive, little feedback, promotes memory-level learning, ignores individual differences, boring if long. Lecture + demonstration/discussion is better.

### Demonstration method
- Teacher (or expert) **performs** an experiment/process in front of learners while explaining; "seeing is believing". A teaching procedure of **clarification/explanation of a major idea**.
- Best when apparatus is costly/dangerous/scarce or a skill/procedure must be shown (e.g., Boolean circuit, installing OS, how a compiler runs). Learners observe, so less hands-on than lab/individual practical.
- Steps: planning → rehearsal → introduction → performance with explanation → questioning → summary/record.

### Discussion-family methods (group interaction)

| Method | Distinguishing feature |
|---|---|
| **Discussion** | free exchange of ideas among teacher and learners on a topic; teacher moderates |
| **Seminar** | one participant presents a prepared **paper**, then group discusses; deepens understanding at higher level |
| **Symposium** | **several experts each present a different aspect** of one theme (short speeches), then audience questions; no cross-argument among speakers |
| **Panel discussion** | **4-8 experts** discuss a topic among themselves before audience, moderated by a chairperson; audience may join later |
| **Workshop** | participants work together (hands-on) for a period to **produce/learn** a skill or product; learning by doing |
| **Conference** | large formal gathering of delegates; papers + resolutions |
| **Brainstorming** | **Alex Osborn** (1953): group generates **many ideas quickly**, **no criticism/evaluation during idea generation**, quantity then quality; develops creativity/divergent thinking |
| **Buzz session** | class split in small groups (5-6) for 5-10 min on a question |
| **Team teaching** | two or more teachers jointly plan and teach one class |
| **Tutorial** | small group problem-solving under teacher |

### Project method
- Propounded by **W. H. Kilpatrick** (philosophy of **John Dewey**, pragmatism); "a whole-hearted purposeful activity proceeding in a social environment". Learning by doing, problem/real-life based.
- **Stevenson** steps: providing a situation → choosing and purposing → planning → executing → evaluating → recording/reporting.
- Types (Kilpatrick): producer, consumer, problem, drill/skill projects. Merits: motivation, social skills, creativity. Limits: time-consuming, hard to cover syllabus, needs resources. In 5E, project work = **Elaborate**.

### Problem-solving method
- **Dewey's** steps (How We Think): felt difficulty → defining problem → collecting data/suggesting solutions (hypotheses) → testing/reasoning → verifying and concluding. **First step = sensing/feeling the problem.**
- Develops scientific attitude, reflective thinking (reflective level).

### Heuristic method
- **H. E. Armstrong** (Greek *heurisko* = "I find out"). Learner is a discoverer/investigator, teacher gives minimal guidance. Related: Bruner's **discovery learning**, inquiry method.

### Role play, drama, storytelling
- **Role play**: learners **act out** assumed roles/situations spontaneously; develops empathy, communication, decision-making. **Dramatisation** is scripted; **most useful for language** (and history, social science).
- **Story telling**: narrate content as story; stimulates imagination, interest, memory; suitable for **primary classes**; helps make lessons lively.
- **Simulation / game**: imitate a real system (e.g., traffic, network packets) to learn safely.

### Assignment, supervised study, Dalton, etc.
- **Assignment method**: tasks given to be done outside/inside class for independent study; develops self-study habits (quoted: Douglas - "assignment is an important step in teaching-learning").
- **Supervised study**: students study in school under supervision of the teacher who guides (study skills, library use).
- **Dalton Plan**: **Helen Parkhurst** - contract/assignment, laboratory-style, learner chooses pace. **Winnetka plan**: Carleton Washburne. **Montessori**: Maria Montessori. **Kindergarten**: Froebel. **Basic education (Nai Talim)**: Gandhi (learning through craft).
- **Programmed instruction**: **Skinner** (linear, small steps, immediate reinforcement), **Crowder** (branching), **Pressey** (teaching machine). Based on operant conditioning.

### Cooperative / collaborative learning
- Small heterogeneous groups work towards a common goal with **positive interdependence + individual accountability**. Key names: **Johnson & Johnson, Slavin (STAD), Aronson (Jigsaw)**, **Think-Pair-Share (Lyman)**.
- Jigsaw: each member becomes an "expert" on one part and teaches the home group.

### Flipped classroom
- **Content (video/reading) at home, application/problem-solving/discussion in class** - reverse of traditional. Popularised by **Bergmann and Sams**. Teacher becomes facilitator; supports ICT and blended learning.

### Inductive vs deductive

| | Inductive | Deductive |
|---|---|---|
| Path | examples → observation → generalisation/rule | rule/definition → application → examples |
| Maxim | particular to general, concrete to abstract | general to particular |
| Fits | science, grammar, discovery learning | maths, formulae, revision |
| Merits | understanding, active | saves time, quick |
| Associated | Francis Bacon (induction) | Aristotle (deduction) |

### Other commonly asked
- **Socratic method**: question-answer to elicit knowledge. **Dialogue**; **Recitation**: review by questioning. **Laboratory method**: learners themselves perform experiments (more hands-on than demonstration). **Survey/Field trip/excursion**: direct experience. **Case study**: detailed study of one situation/unit. **Textbook method** and **Source method** (history).

**⚡ Exam tip:** Symposium = many speakers, different aspects of ONE theme; Seminar = ONE paper then discussion; Panel = experts discuss among themselves. Project = Kilpatrick; Heuristic = Armstrong; Dalton = Parkhurst; Brainstorming = Osborn (no criticism); drama/role play = best for language.

## 3. Teaching Aids and Media

- **Teaching aids** make teaching concrete, interesting and effective; **"aids", not substitutes for teacher**. Selection criteria: relevance to objective, suitability to age/level, simplicity, availability/cost, accuracy, and ease of use. **Most important criterion = suits the learning objective.**
- Principles: select → prepare → present at the right time → remove when done → evaluate.

### Edgar Dale's Cone of Experience (1946)
```
 (top - most abstract)
  Verbal symbols
  Visual symbols
  Radio, recordings, still pictures
  Motion pictures (TV/film)
  Exhibits
  Field trips
  Demonstrations
  Dramatised (dramatic participation) experiences
  Contrived experiences (models, mock-ups, simulations)
  Direct purposeful experiences
 (base - most concrete)
```
- Dale did **not** claim "learning percentages" (those figures are a myth). Cone moves from concrete to abstract; effective learning uses mix.

### Classification of aids

| Type | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Audio** | radio, tape/audio recorder, gramophone, podcast, language lab, Gyan Vani |
| **Visual - non-projected** | chalkboard, charts, maps, models, posters, flash cards, flannel board, bulletin board, specimens, graphs, cartoons |
| **Visual - projected** | slides, filmstrip, OHP (overhead projector), **epidiascope** (opaque, e.g., book page/picture), LCD projector |
| **Audio-visual** | television, film/motion picture, video, computer multimedia, smart class, YouTube, drama |
| **Activity/Hands-on** | field trip, experiments, models, simulations |

- **Epidiascope**: projects opaque objects/pictures as well as transparencies. **OHP**: transparencies, projected visual aid. **Blackboard**: oldest, most-used, cheapest, non-projected visual aid. **Radio** = purely audio; **TV** = audio-visual.
- **Display board/flannel board/model** = non-projected. Explaining a stack with a **pile of plates** = use of **analogy/concrete illustration**.
- **Software-based aids**: PowerPoint, simulations, animations, educational games, virtual labs (e.g., Scratch, GeoGebra, PhET).

**⚡ Exam tip:** Dale's cone base = direct purposeful experience; top = verbal symbols. Radio = audio; OHP/slide = projected visual; chart/map/model = non-projected; TV/film = audio-visual.

## 4. Lesson Planning

### Importance
- Systematic, saves time, gives confidence, clear objectives, proper use of aids, enables evaluation. A good plan is **flexible**, objective-based, learner-centred, realistic and written in advance.
- **Instructional objectives**: written in **behavioural / measurable terms** ("the learner will *define, list, draw, write, calculate*"); avoid vague verbs (*know, understand, appreciate, realise*).
- **Mager's ABCD**: **A**udience, **B**ehaviour, **C**ondition, **D**egree (criterion of acceptable performance).
- Objectives are classified by **Bloom's taxonomy** (below): knowledge, understanding, application, skill.

### Bloom's taxonomy
- Cognitive domain (Bloom 1956): Knowledge → Comprehension → Application → Analysis → Synthesis → Evaluation.
- **Revised (Anderson & Krathwohl 2001)**: Remember → Understand → Apply → Analyse → Evaluate → **Create** (highest).
- Affective (Krathwohl 1964): receiving, responding, valuing, organisation, characterisation. Psychomotor (Simpson/Dave/Harrow): perception, set, guided response, mechanism, complex overt response, adaptation, origination.

### Herbartian steps (five formal steps; **Herbart** - Johann Friedrich Herbart; developed by his followers Ziller & Rein)

| Step | Activity |
|---|---|
| 1. **Preparation** (introduction) | test previous knowledge, motivate, announce aim |
| 2. **Presentation** | present new content with aids |
| 3. **Association / Comparison** | link with known facts, compare/contrast |
| 4. **Generalisation** (Systematisation) | draw rule/conclusion |
| 5. **Application** | apply rule to new problems (last step) |
- **Morrison's** five steps (Unit plan): Exploration → Presentation → Assimilation → Organisation → Recitation. Testing previous knowledge at the start = Preparation (Herbart) / Exploration (Morrison).
- **Glaser's basic teaching model**: instructional objectives → entering behaviour → instructional procedure → performance assessment (with feedback loop).

### 5E instructional model (Bybee, BSCS; constructivist)

| E | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Engage** | arouse curiosity, assess prior knowledge, pose question |
| **Explore** | hands-on investigation, minimal teacher direction |
| **Explain** | concepts/terms explained, students share understanding |
| **Elaborate** (Extend) | apply to new situations; **project-based activity** |
| **Evaluate** | assess understanding, reflect |
- 7E adds Elicit and Extend. Learning-cycle order: Engage → Explore → Explain → Elaborate → Evaluate.
- **EATS model**: **E**ssential questions, **A**ctivating prior knowledge, **T**eaching, **S**ummarising & assessment.

### Other lesson-plan types
- **Unit plan** (Morrison), **Year plan**, **Daily/lesson plan**. **Constructivist 5E plan** vs Herbartian. **Annual → Unit → Lesson**. Duration of a period in school: ~40-45 min.
- Lesson plan components: general information (class, subject, topic, date, duration), general and specific objectives, previous knowledge, teaching aids, introduction, presentation, recapitulation/questions, blackboard summary, assignment/homework.

**⚡ Exam tip:** Herbart order = Preparation, Presentation, Association (Comparison), Generalisation, Application; last = Application. 5E: Engage, Explore, Explain, Elaborate, Evaluate - project work = Elaborate. Objectives should use observable action verbs.

## 5. Microteaching and Simulation

- **Microteaching**: a **scaled-down, simplified, teacher-training technique** (small lesson, few students, short time, single skill) with immediate feedback. **Developed at Stanford University (USA)** by **Dwight Allen** (with Keith Acheson, Robert Bush) in **1963**. In India introduced by **D. D. Tiwari** (Baroda, 1967) and popularised by **B. K. Passi**.
- Microteaching is **real teaching but in a constructed situation** (real students, real teaching, scaled down); "teaching reduced in complexity".
- Typical scale: **5-10 minutes** lesson, **5-10 pupils**, **one skill** at a time, one concept.

### Cycle (Teach - Re-teach), total **36 min** (Indian model)
```
Plan → Teach (6 min) → Feedback (6 min) → Re-plan (12 min) → Re-teach (6 min) → Re-feedback (6 min)
```
- Steps order: **Plan → Teach → Feedback → Re-plan → Re-teach → Re-feedback**. In some texts teach = 5-7 min, feedback 5-6, re-plan 10-12.
- Phases (Allen): knowledge acquisition (discussion, modelling) → skill acquisition (practice) → transfer.

### Teaching skills (Stanford/Passi)

| Skill | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Set induction** (introduction) | prepare class mentally to receive the lesson (arouse interest, link to prior knowledge) |
| **Closure** | consolidate/summarise at end; link learning to future; recapitulation |
| **Stimulus variation** | vary voice, gestures, movement, pauses, focussing, interaction styles to hold attention |
| **Probing questions** | prompt for deeper/complete answers through follow-up questions (prompting, redirecting, seeking clarification, refocussing) |
| **Fluency in questioning** | asking questions smoothly, clearly, level-appropriate |
| **Reinforcement** | praise, nod, "Very good" - strengthening desirable responses (positive verbal/non-verbal) |
| **Explaining** | clear, logical, connected statements, use of examples |
| **Illustrating with examples** | inductive/deductive examples, simple to complex |
| **Use of blackboard** | legible, organised writing/diagrams |
| **Silence and non-verbal cues** | purposeful pauses, gestures |
| **Recognising attending behaviour** | notice if learners are attentive |
| **Achieving closure** | as above |
- Giving praise ("Very good" + nod) = **reinforcement**; varying voice/gesture/movement/pause = **stimulus variation**; asking follow-up to get deeper answer = **probing questioning**; link with previous knowledge at the start = **set induction**; ending/summarising = **closure**.
- **Supervisor/peers give feedback** using observation schedules/checklists, video/audio recording; supervisor-trainee interaction.

### Simulated teaching / Simulation
- **Simulation**: artificial imitation of real teaching (role-play) before actual classroom, e.g., **simulated social skill training (SST)** - trainees play roles of teacher, students, observers. Reduces fear; **no real students**.
- Related: **Team teaching, Peer teaching, Interaction analysis (Flanders' FIAC - 10 categories: 7 teacher talk, 2 student talk, 1 silence)**, **Stimulated recall**.
- **Practice teaching / internship** is real classroom teaching (longer, multiple skills).

**⚡ Exam tip:** Microteaching = Stanford, Dwight Allen; 5-10 min, 5-10 pupils, one skill; cycle Plan-Teach-Feedback-Re-plan-Re-teach-Re-feedback (36 min). Do not confuse **simulation** (role-play among peers) with microteaching (real small class).

## 6. The Teacher: Qualities, Roles and Ethics

- **Qualities**: command over subject, communication skill, patience, empathy, impartiality, emotional stability, **emotional intelligence (Daniel Goleman)** - ability to perceive, understand and manage one's own and others' emotions, enthusiasm, discipline, love for children, professional commitment, lifelong learning, use of ICT. The **most important quality** of a good teacher = **subject knowledge combined with ability to communicate (love of teaching/student concern)** - the answer in most papers is the option that blends knowledge + communication + concern for learners.
- **Roles**: facilitator, guide, motivator, mentor, counsellor, assessor, researcher, planner, manager, role model, community link.
- **Leadership styles (Lewin)**: **autocratic** (teacher decides all), **democratic** (shared decision, best learning climate), **laissez-faire** (no control). A democratic teacher encourages participation and respects ideas.
- **Pygmalion (Rosenthal) effect**: students perform according to teacher's expectations (self-fulfilling prophecy). **Halo effect**: general impression colours specific judgements.
- Primary teacher should concentrate on **all-round development**.
- **Teachers' Day**: 5 September (birth of Dr S. Radhakrishnan).

### Professional ethics
- Honesty, punctuality, impartiality (no discrimination of caste, religion, gender), confidentiality of student records, **no corporal punishment** (prohibited by **RTE Act 2009, Sec 17**), **no private tuition to own students**, fair assessment, no misuse of authority/power, respect for parents/colleagues, continuous professional development (**NEP 2020: 50 hours CPD per year**), obey code of conduct, no exploitation of students, develop values.
- Unethical: favouritism, leaking question papers, harassing/humiliating students, partiality in marking, forcing tuition.
- Professional development is promoted by **reflective practice, action research, workshops/seminars, in-service training, peer observation, reading, ICT use, NISHTHA training**.
- **Learner-centred** attitude = teacher respects individual differences (**Gardner**: multiple intelligences, **Gifted > IQ 130**).

**⚡ Exam tip:** Choose "all-round development / love and respect for students / impartiality / lifelong learning" in ethics-type items; "corporal punishment" is prohibited (RTE 2009); democratic style is best.

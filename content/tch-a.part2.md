
## 4. Motivation, Memory, Forgetting & Transfer

### 4.1 Motivation

**Motivation** = internal state that arouses, directs and sustains behaviour toward a goal. Parts: need → drive → goal (incentive).

| Type | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Intrinsic** | From within; interest, joy, curiosity | Reads novels just for the joy of reading |
| **Extrinsic** | From outside; reward, grades, praise, punishment | Studies only to get a promised gift |

- Intrinsic motivation gives deeper, lasting learning; excess external rewards can undermine it (overjustification).
- **Theories**: Maslow (hierarchy); **McClelland** (need for achievement, n-Ach, affiliation, power); **Weiner** attribution theory (ability, effort, task difficulty, luck; stable/unstable, internal/external); **Atkinson** achievement motivation; **Hull** drive reduction; **Bandura** self-efficacy; **Self-determination** (Deci and Ryan: autonomy, competence, relatedness); **Goal-setting**.
- **Level of aspiration** (Lewin): the goal a person sets for his future performance. **Zeigarnik effect**: unfinished tasks are remembered better.
- Classroom techniques: clear goals, interest, praise, knowledge of results (feedback), healthy competition and cooperation, success experiences, variety, link with real life, appropriate challenge, and a positive classroom climate. Praise generally works better than blame; reproof works better than being ignored.
- **Attention**: selective focusing of consciousness. Factors: objective (size, intensity, novelty, movement, contrast) and subjective (interest, need, set, habit). **Interest**: tendency to attend to something; "interest is the child's best teacher".

### 4.2 Memory

Four steps: **Encoding (learning) → Storage (retention) → Retrieval (recall/recognition) → (Forgetting)**.

**Atkinson-Shiffrin (multi-store) model (1968)**

| Store | Duration | Capacity |
|---|---|---|
| **Sensory memory** | iconic (visual) about 0.5 s; echoic (auditory) 3-4 s | Large |
| **Short-term memory (STM)** | about 15-30 s without rehearsal | **7 ± 2 items** (George Miller's "magical number seven"); **chunking** increases it |
| **Long-term memory (LTM)** | Years to lifetime | Unlimited |

- Rehearsal moves information from STM to LTM. Baddeley and Hitch: **working memory** (central executive, phonological loop, visuospatial sketchpad, episodic buffer).
- LTM kinds: **Explicit/declarative** (episodic: personal events; semantic: facts) and **implicit/procedural** (skills like cycling).
- Craik and Lockhart: **levels of processing**; deeper (meaning-based) processing gives better retention.
- Recall vs recognition: recognition (MCQ) is easier than recall (essay/fill-in); **relearning** (savings method) is the most sensitive measure. Methods: recall, recognition, relearning (**Ebbinghaus**).
- Improving memory: understanding (meaningful), organisation, **mnemonics** (acronyms, loci, keyword), spaced vs massed practice (spaced is better), **whole vs part method** (whole better for short/meaningful material), overlearning, **recitation** (self-testing), good sleep, **SQ3R** (Survey, Question, Read, Recite, Review; by F.P. Robinson).
- Serial position: primacy and recency effects (items at beginning and end are recalled best).

### 4.3 Forgetting

- **Ebbinghaus** (German): nonsense syllables; **forgetting curve** shows rapid loss early (most forgetting occurs soon after learning, then the curve levels off).
- Theories:

| Theory | Idea |
|---|---|
| **Decay / disuse** | Memory traces fade with time (Thorndike's law of disuse) |
| **Interference** | **Proactive** (old learning disrupts new), **Retroactive** (new learning disrupts old) |
| **Repression / motivated forgetting** | Freud: painful memories pushed into the unconscious |
| **Retrieval failure** | Info is stored but cue is missing (tip-of-the-tongue) |
| **Organic/amnesia** | Brain damage; **retrograde** (past events lost) vs **anterograde** (cannot form new memories) |

- Minimising forgetting: meaningful learning, revision at spaced intervals, avoid interference (sleep after learning), use of cues, over-learning.

### 4.4 Transfer of Learning

| Type | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Positive** | Earlier learning helps new (maths helps physics) |
| **Negative** | Earlier learning hinders new (typing on QWERTY vs another layout; driving on left vs right) |
| **Zero** | No effect |
| **Horizontal / Vertical** | Similar level / from lower-level skill to higher-level skill |

- **Thorndike**: *identical elements* theory. **Judd**: *generalisation* (principles). **Gestalt**: transposition. **Formal discipline** (mental faculties strengthened by training) is now discredited.
- Teachers promote transfer by teaching principles, giving varied examples, linking subjects.

**⚡ Exam tip:** STM capacity = 7 ± 2 (Miller); forgetting curve = Ebbinghaus; proactive = old hurts new, retroactive = new hurts old. Intrinsic vs extrinsic: "studying to get a gift" = extrinsic. Savings (relearning) method is the most sensitive measure of retention.

## 5. Intelligence, Creativity & Personality

### 5.1 Intelligence: Definitions and Tests

- **Binet**: judgement, good sense, initiative; **Wechsler**: global capacity to act purposefully, think rationally and deal effectively with the environment; **Terman**: ability to think abstractly; **Gardner**: ability to solve problems or create products valued in a culture.
- **Alfred Binet and Simon (1905)** produced the first intelligence test (France) → concept of **mental age (MA)**. **Terman** (Stanford-Binet, 1916) used **IQ**. **William Stern** (German) proposed the IQ ratio.

**IQ formula (ratio IQ)**

```
IQ = (Mental Age / Chronological Age) × 100
```

Worked examples
- CA = 10 yrs, MA = 12 yrs → IQ = 12/10 × 100 = **120**.
- IQ = 75, CA = 16 yrs → MA = IQ × CA / 100 = 75 × 16 / 100 = **12 years**.
- MA = 8, CA = 10 → IQ = 80 (dull).
- CA = 8, IQ = 125 → MA = 10 yrs. If MA = CA, IQ = 100 (average).
- Convert months: CA = 10 y 6 m = 126 months; MA = 12 y 6 m = 150 months; IQ = 150/126 × 100 ≈ 119.
- **Deviation IQ** (Wechsler): mean 100, SD 15 (Stanford-Binet SD = 16). About 68% of people lie within IQ 85-115.

**Terman's classification of IQ**

| IQ | Category |
|---|---|
| 140 and above | Genius / near genius |
| 120-139 | Very superior |
| 110-119 | Superior |
| 90-109 | Average / normal |
| 80-89 | Dull normal |
| 70-79 | Borderline |
| Below 70 | Intellectual disability (mental retardation); 50-69 **moron** (mild), 25-49 **imbecile** (moderate/severe), below 25 **idiot** (profound) |

- A child with **IQ 130 or above** is usually called **gifted** (many texts: 130+; Terman: 140+). Slow learners: IQ 70-90. 

**Tests**: Verbal (Binet, Wechsler WAIS/WISC, Army Alpha), **Non-verbal / Performance** (Army Beta, Koh's Block Design, Alexander's Passalong, Pintner-Patterson), **Culture-fair** (**Raven's Progressive Matrices**, Cattell's Culture-Fair test, Goodenough Draw-a-Man test). Group vs individual tests. In India: Jalota's group test, Kuppuswamy, Bhatia battery (performance).

### 5.2 Theories of Intelligence

| Theorist | Theory | Key points |
|---|---|---|
| **Spearman (1904)** | **Two-factor** | **g** (general) + **s** (specific); g is common to all tasks |
| **Thorndike** | Multifactor/ social | Many specific bonds; abstract, mechanical and social intelligence |
| **Thurstone** | **Primary Mental Abilities** (7) | Verbal comprehension, word fluency, number, space, memory, perceptual speed, reasoning (no g) |
| **Cattell** | Fluid (Gf) & Crystallised (Gc) | Gf innate reasoning; Gc acquired knowledge |
| **Vernon** | Hierarchical | g → major group factors (verbal-educational, practical-mechanical) → minor |
| **Guilford (1967)** | **Structure of Intellect (SI)** | 3 dimensions: **Operations** (5: cognition, memory, divergent production, convergent production, evaluation) × **Contents** (4 at first: figural, symbolic, semantic, behavioural) × **Products** (6: units, classes, relations, systems, transformations, implications) = **120 factors** (5×4×6; later 150 with 5 contents) |
| **Gardner (1983)** | **Multiple Intelligences** | 8 types (see below) |
| **Sternberg** | **Triarchic** | Analytical (componential), Creative (experiential), Practical (contextual) |
| **Goleman (1995)** | **Emotional Intelligence** | Self-awareness, self-regulation, motivation, empathy, social skills (earlier model: Salovey and Mayer, 1990) |

**Gardner's 8 intelligences**: Linguistic, Logical-mathematical, Spatial, Bodily-kinesthetic, Musical, Interpersonal, Intrapersonal, **Naturalist** (8th, added 1996-99). (Existential: "half" candidate.) Implication: teach in varied ways; a child may be strong in one area.

- **EQ**: ability to recognise, understand and manage one's own and others' emotions. High EQ predicts success.
- Cattell's 16PF and Eysenck also relate to personality (below).
- **Heredity vs environment** in intelligence: both; twin studies show high correlation for identical twins; IQ is fairly stable after childhood.
- **Flynn effect**: IQ scores rose over generations.

### 5.3 Creativity

- **Creativity** = ability to produce something original, novel and useful. **Guilford**: closely related to **divergent thinking** (many answers; open-ended) as opposed to **convergent thinking** (one right answer; conventional tests). Creativity is not the same as intelligence; after a threshold IQ (about 120) the relationship weakens (Getzels and Jackson: creativity vs intelligence).
- **Torrance Tests of Creative Thinking (TTCT)**: four components

| Component | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Fluency** | Number of ideas | 20 uses of a brick → count |
| **Flexibility** | Variety of categories | Ideas from many different categories |
| **Originality** | Uncommon/novel ideas | Rare responses |
| **Elaboration** | Details added | Embellishing an idea |

- "A student lists 20 possible uses of a brick" = **fluency** (divergent thinking). "Ideas from many categories" = **flexibility**.
- **Stages of the creative process (Wallas, 1926)**: **Preparation → Incubation → Illumination → Verification** (Aha at illumination).
- Tests: Guilford's Unusual Uses/Consequences, Mednick's Remote Associates Test, Torrance (verbal and figural), Passi's Test of Creativity (India), Baqer Mehdi's.
- Fostering creativity: brainstorming (**Alex Osborn**: defer judgement, quantity), open-ended questions, freedom and psychological safety, problem-solving, projects, avoiding rigid discipline and over-correction. Other methods: synectics (Gordon), lateral thinking (de Bono; "six thinking hats").
- Characteristics of creative persons: curiosity, independence, non-conformity, sense of humour, intuition, tolerance of ambiguity.

### 5.4 Personality

- **Allport**: "Personality is the dynamic organisation within the individual of those psychophysical systems that determine his unique adjustments to his environment." (Word "persona" = mask.)
- **Type theories**: Hippocrates (4 humours: sanguine, phlegmatic, choleric, melancholic); **Kretschmer** (pyknic, athletic, asthenic); **Sheldon** (endomorph = viscerotonic, mesomorph = somatotonic, ectomorph = cerebrotonic); **Jung** (**introvert / extrovert**; ambivert is a mix, proposed later); **Friedman and Rosenman** (Type A/B).
- **Trait theories**: **Allport** (cardinal, central, secondary traits); **Cattell** (16 source traits, **16PF**); **Eysenck** (PEN: Psychoticism, Extraversion, Neuroticism); **Big Five / OCEAN** (Costa and McCrae): Openness, Conscientiousness, Extraversion, Agreeableness, Neuroticism.
- **Psychoanalytic**: Freud (id, ego, superego), Jung (collective unconscious), Adler (inferiority complex, striving for superiority; individual psychology), Erikson.
- **Humanistic**: **Rogers** (self-concept; real self vs ideal self; congruence), Maslow.
- **Social-learning**: Bandura (reciprocal determinism).

**Personality assessment**

| Category | Tests |
|---|---|
| **Projective** (unstructured, unconscious) | **Rorschach Inkblot** (10 cards; Hermann Rorschach); **TAT** (Thematic Apperception Test; Murray and Morgan, 1935; 30 picture cards + 1 blank); **CAT** (children, Bellak; animal figures); **Word Association** (Jung/Galton); **Sentence Completion**; **Draw-a-Person**; Rosenzweig Picture Frustration (P-F) study |
| **Objective / self-report** (inventories) | **MMPI**; **16PF**; **EPI** (Eysenck Personality Inventory); NEO-PI; Bernreuter; Bell Adjustment Inventory |
| Others | Observation, interview, rating scale, case history, sociometry (**Moreno**: peer relations), anecdotal records, cumulative record card |

- Sociometry: shows who is popular ("star"), isolates and rejected children.

**⚡ Exam tip:** IQ = MA/CA × 100 (check units: both in years or both in months). Guilford SI = 120 factors; Gardner = 8; Thurstone = 7; Spearman = g + s; Sternberg = triarchic; Goleman = EI. Torrance = fluency, flexibility, originality, elaboration. TAT = Murray; Rorschach = 10 inkblots. Introvert/extrovert = Jung.

## 6. Adolescence, Individual Differences & Exceptional Children

### 6.1 Adolescence

- From Latin *adolescere* = "to grow into maturity". Roughly **12-18/19 years** (puberty about 11-14; girls earlier). Includes early (12-14), mid (15-17) and late adolescence.
- **G. Stanley Hall**: adolescence is a period of **"storm and stress"** (*Sturm und Drang*); **Kurt Lewin**: marginal man; **Margaret Mead** (Samoa): culture matters; **Erikson**: identity vs role confusion; **Piaget**: formal operations; Kohlberg: conventional morality; Freud: genital stage.
- **Puberty**: onset of sexual maturity with rapid physical changes; **primary** sex characteristics (reproductive organs) and **secondary** (voice, hair, breasts), **growth spurt** (peak velocity).
- Characteristics: rapid physical growth, emotional instability and mood swings, identity search, peer influence, hero worship, rebellion against authority, idealism, abstract reasoning, interest in opposite sex, heightened imagination, day-dreaming, self-consciousness.
- Problems: identity crisis, peer pressure, substance abuse, delinquency, eating disorders, exam stress, internet addiction, depression, suicidal ideation, conflict with parents ("generation gap").
- Guidance: sex/health education (life-skills), sympathetic non-judgemental handling, creative outlets, sports, NSS/NCC/Scouts, guidance and counselling, treating them as responsible, parent-teacher dialogue; avoid harsh criticism.
- Juvenile delinquency: anti-social acts by those below 18 (Juvenile Justice Act 2015). Causes: poor home, bad company, poverty, lack of love.

### 6.2 Individual Differences

- No two individuals are alike: **intra-individual** (within the person: good in maths, weak in language) and **inter-individual** (between persons). Causes: heredity, environment (home, school, culture, nutrition), maturity, sex, age, socio-economic status.
- Areas: physical, intelligence, interest, aptitude, attitude, personality, motivation, achievement, learning style, emotional.
- Educational provisions:
  - Flexible curriculum and varied teaching methods, **differentiated instruction** (varying content, process, product, environment per learner).
  - **Homogeneous grouping**, remedial teaching, enrichment, acceleration (skipping), tutorials, individual (Dalton Plan, Montessori, Winnetka, **Programmed learning**), **peer tutoring**, co-curricular activities, guidance/counselling, continuous assessment.
  - Use of multiple intelligences; **learning styles**: visual (diagrams, charts), auditory, kinaesthetic (VAK); Kolb, Honey-Mumford.
- **Aptitude** = potential to learn a specific skill; **interest** = liking; **attitude** = predisposition to react; **achievement** = what is learned.

### 6.3 Gifted, Slow Learners, Backward & Learning Disabled

| Category | Features | Provisions |
|---|---|---|
| **Gifted** (IQ ≥ 130; Terman: ≥ 140) | Fast learning, wide interests, high curiosity, reasoning, leadership; may be bored, may be socially isolated | **Enrichment** (best within regular class), **acceleration** (double promotion), special classes/ability grouping, mentoring, **Navodaya**, NTSE, advanced projects. **Renzulli**: giftedness = above-average ability + task commitment + creativity (three rings) |
| **Slow learner** (IQ about 70-90) | Below-average learning speed but can learn | Remedial teaching, concrete aids, repetition, patience, individual attention |
| **Backward child** | Achievement below expectation (educational backwardness); may have normal IQ; AQ < 85 | Remove cause (poor attendance, health, home), remedial, motivation. |
| **Mentally challenged** | IQ < 70 | Special schools, vocational training, IEP |
| **Delinquent / maladjusted** | Antisocial, truancy | Guidance, counselling, reformative approach |
| **Creative child** | Divergent thinking | Freedom, open-ended tasks |

**Specific learning disabilities (SLD)**: normal intelligence but difficulty in particular academic skills; not due to low IQ, poor teaching or sensory defects.

| Term | Difficulty in |
|---|---|
| **Dyslexia** | **Reading** (decoding words; letter reversals b/d; spelling) |
| **Dysgraphia** | **Writing** (handwriting, expressing thoughts in writing) |
| **Dyscalculia** | **Mathematics** (numbers, calculations) |
| **Dyspraxia** | Motor coordination/planning |
| **Dysphasia / aphasia** | Speech and language |
| **ADHD** | Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder: inattention, hyperactivity, impulsivity (onset before about age 12) |
| **Autism spectrum disorder (ASD)** | Neurodevelopmental: difficulty in social communication and interaction, restricted and repetitive behaviours. Term by **Leo Kanner (1943)**; Asperger (higher functioning). Early signs by age 2-3 |

- Management: early identification, multi-sensory teaching (VAKT: **Fernald**), Individualised Education Plan (**IEP**), assistive technology, extra time, scribes, non-punitive handling, collaboration with parents and counsellor.
- Other disabilities: visual (Braille, large print), hearing (sign language, hearing aids), locomotor, speech, intellectual, cerebral palsy.

### 6.4 Inclusive & Special Education

- **Special education**: separate, tailored education in special schools. **Integrated education**: children with disabilities placed in regular school but with minimal change. **Inclusive education**: all children learn together in the same classroom, the school adapts to the child (the child need not adapt).
- Policy milestones: **IEDC** (Integrated Education for Disabled Children, 1974); **NPE 1986/POA 1992**; **PIED**; **Rehabilitation Council of India Act 1992** (trains special educators); **PWD Act 1995**; **Salamanca Statement (1994)** (inclusive education, Spain); **UNCRPD 2006**; **RTE 2009** (children with disabilities included in disadvantaged group); **IEDSS** (2009); **RPwD Act 2016** (replaced PWD 1995; **21 disabilities** recognised; reservation of **4%** in government jobs and **5%** in higher education; free education for **6-18** years for children with benchmark disabilities (40% or more)); Samagra Shiksha integrates disability; NEP 2020 stresses inclusion, **Divyang** (Rights of PwD).
- Teacher's role: positive attitude, differentiated teaching, universal design for learning (**UDL**), resource room, peer support, barrier-free environment, flexible assessment.

**⚡ Exam tip:** Dyslexia = reading; dysgraphia = writing/handwriting; dyscalculia = maths. Gifted: **enrichment in the regular class** is the best provision; acceleration is the other. The adolescent period is called "storm and stress" (Hall); central task = identity (Erikson). RPwD Act 2016 = 21 disabilities.


## 7. Learning Theories and the Learner (Quick Facts)

| Theory | Key name | Core idea |
|---|---|---|
| **Classical conditioning** | **Pavlov** | UCS (food) → UCR (salivation); bell is a **neutral stimulus** that becomes **conditioned stimulus (CS)** after pairing → CR |
| **Trial and error / connectionism** | **Thorndike** | laws: **readiness, exercise, effect**; cat in puzzle box |
| **Operant conditioning** | **B. F. Skinner** | behaviour shaped by **reinforcement** (consequences); Skinner box; programmed instruction |
| **Insight learning** | Kohler (Gestalt) | sudden grasp of whole situation (chimp Sultan) |
| **Social/observational** | **Bandura** | learning by imitation/modelling (Bobo doll) |
| **Cognitive development** | **Piaget** | sensorimotor (0-2), preoperational (2-7), **concrete operational (7-11)**, formal operational (11+) |
| **Socio-cultural** | **Vygotsky** | **ZPD** = gap between what learner can do **alone** and what can do **with help** (scaffolding, MKO) |
| **Discovery / spiral** | **Bruner** | enactive-iconic-symbolic; **spiral curriculum** |
| **Constructivism** | Piaget, Vygotsky, Dewey | learner constructs knowledge; NCF 2005 basis |
| **Hierarchy of needs** | Maslow | physiological → safety → love/belonging → esteem → self-actualisation |
| **Multiple intelligences** | **Gardner** | linguistic, logical-mathematical, spatial, musical, bodily-kinesthetic, interpersonal, intrapersonal, naturalistic |
| **Emotional intelligence** | Goleman | self-awareness, self-regulation, motivation, empathy, social skills |

- **IQ = (MA / CA) × 100**. Worked: MA 12, CA 10 → IQ = 12/10 × 100 = **120**.
- **Creativity** is related to **divergent thinking** (Guilford); convergent thinking = single correct answer.
- **Alternate conceptions/misconceptions** in young learners arise from family/community, textbooks, stories/poems, teachers - so all sources count.
- Transfer of learning, motivation (intrinsic > extrinsic), individual differences, readiness, reinforcement are standard principles.

**⚡ Exam tip:** Piaget 7-11 = concrete operational; ZPD = Vygotsky; operant = Skinner; bell before conditioning = neutral stimulus, after = conditioned stimulus; creativity = divergent thinking.

## 8. Curriculum

- **Curriculum** (Latin *currere* = "to run", race-course/path): the **total programme of learning experiences** (academic + co-curricular + activities) planned by the school for the all-round development of learners. Curriculum is to education what a **constitution is to a country** (basic framework).
- **Syllabus** = list of topics/units of a subject for a course/exam.

| Curriculum | Syllabus |
|---|---|
| broad, whole programme of experiences | narrow, list of topics for one subject |
| includes co-curricular, methods, evaluation | content only, exam oriented |
| prescribed by policy bodies (NCF) | prepared by board/teachers for a subject |
| long-term, flexible, dynamic | short-term, fixed per session |

### Principles of curriculum construction
Child-centredness, **community-centredness**, activity/experience, flexibility, **integration/correlation**, utility/vocational relevance, balance, **conservation and creativity** (preserve culture yet innovate), forward-looking (future-oriented), **variety/individual differences**, **democratic values**, **continuity/sequence (spiral)**, and **cultural relevance**.

### Types of curriculum

| Type | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Subject-centred** | organised around disciplines; teacher/textbook oriented |
| **Child-centred (activity)** | based on interests/needs of learners (Dewey, Montessori) |
| **Core curriculum** | **common compulsory** subjects/experiences needed by **all** learners (e.g., languages, maths, science, social science), other subjects as electives around it |
| **Integrated curriculum** | **subject boundaries removed**; content from different subjects combined around themes/problems for unified knowledge (e.g., EVS, general science, STEM) |
| **Hidden curriculum** (Jackson, 1968 - *Life in Classrooms*) | **unwritten, unofficial lessons** - values, attitudes, norms learned from school culture, teacher behaviour, peer interaction, e.g., punctuality, discipline, respect, gender bias |
| **Null curriculum** (Eisner) | what the school **does NOT teach** (omitted subjects/topics/ideas) |
| **Spiral curriculum** (**Bruner**) | same concept revisited at increasing depth/complexity at successive levels |
| **Formal/Overt (written)** | official; **Informal (co-curricular)**; **Operational (taught) / Received (learned)** |
| **Broad-fields / Correlated** | related subjects taught in coordination (e.g., maths + physics) |
| **Learner-/Competency-based** | outcomes in terms of competencies |
- Example of hidden curriculum: students learn **time-discipline, respect for hierarchy, competition** from school routine; a teacher's gender-biased remarks. **Talking about equality in lessons** is explicit (formal) curriculum.
- **Purpose of integrated curriculum**: unify knowledge, show interrelationship, reduce subject load, relate to real life.

### Curriculum development process
Needs assessment → aims/objectives → selection of content → organisation (sequence, scope) → selection of methods/aids → implementation → **evaluation and revision** (feedback loop). **Ralph Tyler** four questions: objectives; learning experiences; organisation; evaluation. Hilda Taba - grassroots, teacher-involved ("bottom-up").

### National Curriculum Frameworks

| | NCF 2005 | NCF-SE 2023 (under NEP 2020) |
|---|---|---|
| Chair | **Prof. Yash Pal** (earlier "Learning Without Burden", 1993, also Yash Pal) | **K. Kasturirangan** (NSTC) |
| Principles | **constructivism**, reduce rote learning, connect knowledge to life, exams flexible/less stressful, textbook not the only resource, **inclusive**, democratic values | **5+3+3+4** stages (Foundational, Preparatory, Middle, Secondary), **flexible subject choice** (no hard arts/science/commerce divide), **Board exams twice a year (option)**, multilingualism, **competency-based**, **experiential learning**, 360° holistic report card, vocational/skills, Indian knowledge, no rote |
| Notes | 21 national focus-group position papers; child's voice and experience | Prepared under NCERT (released 2023); basis for new NEP-aligned textbooks |
- **Learning Without Burden (1993)**: Chair **Prof. Yash Pal**; reduce curriculum load, textbook weight, make learning joyful.
- **Body that develops NCF and model textbooks = NCERT.**
- **Kothari Commission (1964-66)**: 10+2+3 structure. **Mudaliar Commission (1952-53)**: secondary education. **Radhakrishnan Commission (1948-49)**: university education. **NPE 1986** (Programme of Action 1992): Operation Blackboard, Navodaya, **common core**. **NPE 1968**: three-language formula.

**⚡ Exam tip:** Curriculum = currere (race-course), total learning experiences; syllabus = topic list. Core = compulsory for all; Integrated = no subject boundaries; Hidden = unwritten values learned; Null = what is not taught; Spiral = Bruner. NCF 2005 = Yash Pal, constructivism.

## 9. Evaluation, Measurement and Assessment

### Basic terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Measurement** | **quantitative** description of a trait ("How much?") - scores, marks |
| **Assessment** | collection and interpretation of information (quantitative + qualitative) about learning, mainly **for improving** |
| **Evaluation** | **value judgement** about worth/quality of learning/teaching ("How good?") - includes measurement + qualitative + decision |
| Test | an instrument/tool to measure; **Examination** = formal test |
- **Evaluation = Measurement (quantitative) + qualitative description + value judgement.** Evaluation is a **continuous, comprehensive** process and wider than measurement.
- **Stevens' scales**: **Nominal** (names, e.g., roll no.) < **Ordinal** (rank) < **Interval** (equal gaps, no true zero, e.g., Celsius) < **Ratio** (true zero, e.g., length, marks as ratio-scaled).

### Types of assessment

| Type | When | Purpose | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Placement** | before | to find entry behaviour | pre-test, readiness test |
| **Formative** | **during** instruction | improve T-L, give **feedback**, locate gaps (**assessment FOR learning**) | class tests, quizzes, oral questions, assignments, observation |
| **Diagnostic** | during/after | find **specific weaknesses and causes** | diagnostic test |
| **Summative** | **end** of term/course | grading, certification, promotion (**assessment OF learning**) | annual/term exams, board exams |
| **Assessment AS learning** | continuous | learner self-assessment, metacognition | self/peer assessment |
- Formative: low stakes, frequent. Summative: high stakes, final. Scriven coined **formative/summative**; Bloom popularised.

### CCE (Continuous and Comprehensive Evaluation)
- **Continuous** = regular, spread over the year (frequent FA and unit tests); **Comprehensive** = covers **both scholastic and co-scholastic** areas (academic + life skills, attitudes, values, sports, arts, work education).
- Introduced by **CBSE in 2009** (Class X board exam made optional) and **mandated by RTE Act 2009 (Sec 29)** for elementary education; CBSE had 9-point grading (A1-E2). Includes **Formative (FA)** + **Summative (SA)**; grades replace marks; reduces stress.
- Tools: **cumulative record card** (records progress over years), anecdotal record, checklist, rating scale, portfolio, observation, interview, sociometry (Moreno).
- Co-scholastic areas: life skills, attitudes and values, sports/games, literary and creative skills, clubs, health and physical education, work education.
- **NEP 2020**: replaced rote with **competency-based, formative** assessment; **360-degree holistic progress card**; **PARAKH** (National Assessment Centre) for standard-setting; board exams low-stakes, twice a year feasible; assessments in Grades 3, 5, 8.

### Norm-referenced vs criterion-referenced

| | Norm-referenced (NRT) | Criterion-referenced (CRT) |
|---|---|---|
| Compares learner with | other learners (group) | fixed standard/criterion (mastery) |
| Result | rank, percentile | pass/fail, mastery level |
| Use | selection, competitive exams | mastery/diagnosis, driving licence test |
| Example | JEE percentile | "80% = master" test |

### Types of tests
- **Oral, written, practical (performance)**.
- **Essay-type** (subjective): tests organisation, expression, higher thinking; **limitations - low objectivity & reliability, subjective marking, limited sampling, time-consuming**; halo effect.
- **Short-answer**; **Objective-type**: MCQ, true/false, matching, fill-in-the-blank (completion), one-word. Called **objective** because **scoring is independent of the scorer's opinion** (same marks by any examiner).
  - **Selection type** (recognition): MCQ, T/F, matching. **Supply type** (recall): short answer, fill in blanks, completion, essay.
  - MCQ parts: **stem**, **key** (correct option), **distractors** (incorrect options, should be plausible).
- **Standardised vs teacher-made tests**; **Achievement test** (measures what is learnt after instruction), **aptitude test** (future potential), **intelligence test**, **personality**, **interest inventories**, **diagnostic**, **prognostic**.
- **Open book exam**, **online exam**, **grading** (relative vs absolute), **semester system**.
- **Viva voce and practical exam**: test practical skill, understanding, confidence and communication (in computer science - lab work + viva).

### Blueprint (Design) of a question paper
- A **three-dimensional chart** showing **weightage to objectives (knowledge, understanding, application, skill) × content units × forms of question (essay, short, very short/objective)**, plus marks, time and difficulty level. Ensures **content validity**, balance and avoids bias.
- Steps of test construction: **planning (design)** → **blueprint** → writing items → editing → **try-out/item analysis** → final form with marking scheme → administer.

### Characteristics of a good test

| Characteristic | Meaning | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Validity** | measures **what it is meant to measure** | most important; valid → reliable (not reverse) |
| **Reliability** | **consistency/dependability** of scores | reliable need not be valid |
| **Objectivity** | free from examiner's subjective judgement; same scores by different scorers | marker reliability |
| **Usability (practicability)** | easy to administer, score, interpret, economical, time |
| Others | discrimination power, comprehensiveness, difficulty level, norms |

**Types of validity**: **Content** (curriculum validity - adequate sample of syllabus; blueprint helps; "test covering the syllabus adequately has high content validity"), **Face** (looks appropriate), **Criterion-related**: **concurrent** (correlation with current criterion) and **predictive** (correlation with future performance, e.g., entrance test vs later results), **Construct** (measures theoretical trait, e.g., intelligence).

**Methods of estimating reliability**

| Method | Procedure |
|---|---|
| **Test-retest** (stability) | same test twice to same group after gap; correlate (r). Affected by memory/practice |
| **Parallel/equivalent forms** | two equivalent versions, correlate |
| **Split-half** (internal consistency) | one test split into **two halves (odd-even items)**, correlate halves, **Spearman-Brown** correction: r(full) = 2r / (1 + r) |
| **Kuder-Richardson (KR-20, KR-21)** / **Cronbach's α** | internal consistency of items |
| **Inter-rater** | agreement between scorers |
- **Worked example**: half-test correlation r = 0.60 → r(full) = 2×0.60 / 1.60 = **0.75**.
- **Raises reliability**: more items/longer test, clear unambiguous items, objective scoring, standard instructions, moderate difficulty, good discrimination, homogeneous group. **Lowers reliability**: ambiguous questions, too few items, guessing, subjective marking, unclear instructions, cheating, fatigue, too easy/too difficult.
- Weighing scale that always shows **2 kg more** = **reliable (consistent) but not valid** (systematic error).
- A test cannot be valid unless reliable; reliability is necessary but not sufficient.

### Item analysis (difficulty and discrimination)
- Rank students by total score; take **upper group (U) = top 27%** (or top 1/3) and **lower group (L) = bottom 27%**.
```
Difficulty index  P = (RU + RL) / (2n)   [n = size of each group]
                  or P = R / N  (all students; R = correct responses)
Discrimination index D = (RU − RL) / n
```
- **P** ranges 0-1: **higher P = easier**. Good items: P ≈ 0.3-0.7 (acceptable 0.2-0.8). **D** ranges −1 to +1: ≥ 0.4 excellent, 0.3-0.39 good, 0.2-0.29 fair, **< 0.2 poor**, negative = faulty (lower group does better).
- **Example 1**: 40 of 50 students answered correctly → P = R/N = 40/50 = **0.80** (easy item).
- **Example 2**: n = 20 per group; RU = 18, RL = 6 → **D = (18 − 6)/20 = 0.60** (excellent); P = (18+6)/(2×20) = 24/40 = **0.60** (moderate).
- Distractor analysis: a good distractor attracts more from the lower group; non-functional distractor is chosen by nobody.

### Other assessment tools
- **Rating scale**: assigns a numerical/descriptive value to a trait (numerical, graphic, descriptive); judge qualities like behaviour, project quality. **Checklist**: yes/no for presence of a trait. **Anecdotal record**: brief factual note of significant incident. **Sociometry**: social relations (Moreno).
- **Rubric**: a **scoring guide** listing **criteria and performance levels with descriptors** for assessing a task (analytic: criterion-wise; holistic: overall). Improves objectivity/transparency; shared with students.
- **Portfolio**: **purposeful collection of a learner's work over time** showing progress, effort, achievement; supports self-reflection.
- **Cumulative record card**: summarises all-round progress year after year (marks, health, attitude). **Learning outcomes** and **competency-based questions** emphasised in NEP.
- **Descriptive statistics**: mean = ∑x/n. Example: scores 4, 6, 8, 10, 12 → mean = 40/5 = **8**. Median = middle score; mode = most frequent.
- **Grading**: **relative** (bell curve) vs **absolute**; **CGPA**, credit system.

**⚡ Exam tip:** Validity = measures what it should; reliability = consistency; test-retest/parallel/split-half = reliability; content/predictive/construct = validity. D = (U−L)/n; P = (U+L)/2n. Formative = during, for improvement; summative = end, grading. A reliable but invalid weighing scale shows constant error.

## 10. Diagnostic and Remedial Teaching, Learner Differences, Action Research

### Learning difficulties - order of action
**Diagnosis (identify difficulty and cause) → Remedial teaching → Re-evaluation/follow-up.** Steps: identify the learners → locate difficulty (diagnostic test, observation, interview, cumulative record) → find causes (physical, intellectual, emotional, social, instructional) → plan remedy → implement → re-test.
- **Diagnostic test** is **different from an achievement test**: achievement shows how much learnt (overall, broad); diagnostic shows **specific areas of weakness/error and why** (more items on small units, analytical).
- **Remedial teaching**: special, individual/small-group teaching to **slow learners and students with identified difficulties/low achievers** using different methods/aids, extra practice, to bring them to the level of class. **Enrichment programmes** are for **gifted / bright learners** (extend, not accelerate).
- **Acceleration** (skipping grade) is another option for gifted.

### Exceptional learners and disorders

| Term | Note |
|---|---|
| **Gifted** | IQ above **130**; enrichment/acceleration |
| **Slow learner** | IQ approx. 70-85 (below average) |
| **Intellectual disability** | IQ below 70 |
| **Underachiever** | achieves **below the level of ability/potential** |
| **Dyslexia** | difficulty in **reading** |
| **Dysgraphia** | difficulty in **writing/handwriting** |
| **Dyscalculia** | difficulty in **mathematics/numbers** |
| **Dyspraxia** | motor coordination |
| **ADHD** | inattention, hyperactivity, **impulsivity** |
| **Autism spectrum** | social communication and repetitive behaviour |
- **Inclusive education**: all children in same classroom - backed by **RTE 2009**, **RPwD Act 2016** (21 recognised disabilities) and NEP 2020.
- **Individual differences** (heredity, environment, intelligence) require varied methods.

### Action research
- **Action research** = **small-scale, practical research by the practitioner (teacher) to solve an immediate classroom problem and improve own practice.** Term first used by **Kurt Lewin (1946)**; popularised in **education by Stephen Corey (1953, Teachers College Columbia)**. Conducted **by the teacher/practitioner** in the natural setting.
- Cycle (Lewin/Kemmis-McTaggart spiral): **Plan → Act → Observe → Reflect** (then re-plan). Detailed steps: identify problem → analyse/reflect → hypothesis/action plan → implement → collect data/observe → evaluate and reflect → disseminate.
- Features: local, immediate, participatory, flexible, **not meant to generalise**, based on real problems; main purpose = **improve classroom practice/professional growth**.

### Research basics (frequently combined)
- **Hypothesis**: tentative, testable statement of expected relationship between variables (educated guess). **Independent variable** = manipulated/cause (new teaching method); **dependent variable** = effect measured (marks); **extraneous/confounding** variables are controlled.
- **Survey method** = data from a large group by questionnaire/interview (**descriptive**); **experimental method** = manipulation & control (causal); historical, case study, ex-post-facto.
- **Sample** should be **representative, adequate, unbiased** (random/probability sampling); population = whole group.
- Qualitative vs quantitative; reliability and validity of tools apply.

**⚡ Exam tip:** Correct order = diagnosis → remedial teaching → evaluation. Remedial → slow/weak learners; enrichment → gifted. Dysgraphia = writing; dyslexia = reading; dyscalculia = maths. Action research = teacher's own classroom problem, cycle plan-act-observe-reflect (Lewin; Corey in education).

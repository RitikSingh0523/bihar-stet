## 1. Growth, Development & Their Principles

### 1.1 Growth vs Development

| Basis | Growth | Development |
|---|---|---|
| Meaning | Quantitative physical change (height, weight, size) | Qualitative + quantitative; overall change in structure and function |
| Scope | Narrow; one aspect | Wide; physical, mental, social, emotional, moral |
| Ends | Stops at maturity | Continues throughout life (womb to tomb) |
| Measurable | Yes, directly (cm, kg) | Observed and judged; not always measurable |
| Example | Increase in height | Better reasoning, social skills |

- **Maturation**: unfolding of inherited traits, independent of practice (walking, puberty). **Learning**: change through experience and practice.
- Development = Growth + Maturation + Learning (quality of change).
- **Heredity**: transmission of traits through genes. 46 chromosomes (23 pairs) at conception; sex pair XX = female, XY = male. The father's chromosome decides the sex of the child.
- **Environment**: all external forces. The Heredity-Environment issue is "nature vs nurture". Modern view: both interact; heredity sets limits, environment decides how far the child reaches them.
- Twin studies (identical/monozygotic twins) are used to study the relative effect of heredity and environment. Galton: Hereditary Genius, advocated heredity. Watson (behaviourist) stressed environment: "Give me a dozen healthy infants ..."

### 1.2 Principles of Development

- Development is **continuous** (never stops) but the rate is not uniform.
- Development follows a **definite sequence** (crawl → stand → walk).
- **Cephalocaudal**: head to foot. The head and trunk develop before the legs. Baby controls the neck before the legs.
- **Proximodistal**: centre to periphery, trunk-near parts first. Shoulder and arm control before fingers.
- **General to specific**: mass activity to specific responses (grasps with whole hand, then with fingers).
- **Individual differences**: every child develops at their own rate.
- Development is **cumulative** and **interrelated** (physical, mental, social aspects influence one another).
- Development is **predictable** and proceeds from **simple to complex**.
- Early development is more critical; early years are the foundation. Later behaviour is partly predictable from earlier behaviour.
- Both **heredity and environment** contribute (a product of both).
- Development involves **both integration and differentiation**.
- NOT a principle: "development is always uniform / the same for all children / unpredictable".

### 1.3 Stages of Human Development (common school classification)

| Stage | Approx. age |
|---|---|
| Prenatal | Conception to birth (about 9 months/280 days) |
| Infancy | Birth to 2 years |
| Early childhood | 2-6 years ("pre-school" age; Freud: "formative years") |
| Late childhood | 6-12 years (school age; "gang age") |
| Adolescence | 12-18/19 years (puberty onset about 11-14) |
| Adulthood | after 18-20 |

- **Havighurst**: *developmental tasks*, skills and attitudes a person must master at each life stage (walking, reading, accepting one's body, choosing a career).
- Critical periods (sensitive periods): windows in which certain learning occurs most easily; Montessori also uses "sensitive periods".
- Pre-natal: zygote (0-2 weeks) → embryo (2 weeks-2 months) → foetus (2 months-birth).

**⚡ Exam tip:** Growth = quantitative and stops; development = qualitative and lifelong. "Head to foot" = cephalocaudal; "centre to periphery" = proximodistal. A child controlling shoulders/arms before fingers shows proximodistal development.

## 2. Theories of Development

### 2.1 Piaget: Cognitive Development (Swiss; constructivist; "genetic epistemology")

**Key concepts**

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Schema** (scheme) | Mental structure or organised pattern of thought/action; the basic building block |
| **Assimilation** | Fitting new information into an existing schema (child calls a zebra a "horse") |
| **Accommodation** | Changing/creating schema to fit new information (learns the zebra is different) |
| **Equilibration** | Balancing assimilation and accommodation; the drive toward equilibrium; the motor of development |
| **Disequilibrium** | Mental conflict when new information does not fit |
| **Adaptation** | Assimilation + accommodation together |
| **Organisation** | Combining schemas into a coherent system |

**Four stages (fixed order, invariant sequence)**

| Stage | Age | Features |
|---|---|---|
| **Sensorimotor** | 0-2 yrs | Knowing through senses and actions; **object permanence** (achieved about 8-12 months; baby searches for toy hidden under a cloth); circular reactions; means-end behaviour; ends with beginning of symbolic thought |
| **Preoperational** | 2-7 yrs | Symbolic play, language; **egocentrism** (cannot see others' viewpoint; three-mountain task); **animism** (inanimate things are alive); **centration** (focus on one feature); **irreversibility**; **no conservation**; **artificialism**; transductive reasoning |
| **Concrete operational** | 7-11 yrs | Logical thinking about concrete objects; **conservation** (number, mass, volume, liquid), **reversibility**, classification, seriation, decentration; cannot reason on abstract/hypothetical |
| **Formal operational** | 11-12 yrs and up | Abstract, hypothetical-deductive reasoning, scientific/propositional thinking, idealism; solves problems systematically |

- **Conservation** task: water poured from a short wide glass into a tall thin one. A preoperational child says "the tall glass has more". A concrete-operational child says "same", since the child can reverse the action. (Conservation of number is gained about 6-7, volume about 11.)
- Piaget on language: egocentric speech is immature and fades. (Vygotsky disagrees.)
- Piaget's moral development: heteronomous (moral realism, up to about 7-8) → autonomous (moral relativism).
- Educational implication: discovery learning, active learning, readiness, concrete materials for young children, learner-centred approach.

### 2.2 Vygotsky: Socio-cultural Theory (Russian)

- Cognitive development is a social process; language and culture are the main tools. Called **socio-cultural / social constructivism**.
- **Zone of Proximal Development (ZPD)**: the gap between what a learner can do **alone** (actual level) and what he can do **with help** from a more knowledgeable other (potential level).
- **Scaffolding**: temporary support from teacher/peer, gradually withdrawn as the learner becomes independent (term by Wood, Bruner and Ross, applied to Vygotsky's idea).
- **MKO** (More Knowledgeable Other): teacher, parent, peer, even technology.
- **Private speech** (talking to oneself) is a useful tool for thinking and self-regulation; it becomes inner speech. (Piaget: egocentric and immature.)
- Learning leads development (Piaget: development precedes learning).
- Implications: cooperative learning, peer tutoring, reciprocal teaching, guided participation.

| Piaget | Vygotsky |
|---|---|
| Individual constructor, discovery | Social interaction, culture |
| Stages are universal | No fixed stages |
| Development precedes learning | Learning leads development |
| Egocentric speech immature | Private speech is useful |
| Teacher: facilitator of discovery | Teacher: guide, scaffolds |

### 2.3 Kohlberg: Moral Development (3 levels, 6 stages)

Method: moral dilemmas (the **Heinz dilemma**: stealing a drug to save a dying wife). Judged by *reasoning*, not by decision.

| Level | Stage | Orientation | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **I Pre-conventional** (up to about 9 yrs) | 1 | Punishment and obedience | "I'll be punished" |
| | 2 | Instrumental / hedonistic / exchange ("what's in it for me") | "I share to get a gift" |
| **II Conventional** (about 10-20) | 3 | **Good boy-nice girl** / interpersonal conformity | "Others will approve" |
| | 4 | **Law and order**; duty, authority | "Rules keep society in order" |
| **III Post-conventional** (adult; few reach) | 5 | Social contract, democratically agreed laws | "Laws can be changed for greater good" |
| | 6 | Universal ethical principles (justice, human dignity) | Conscience-led |

- Child shares sweets only to avoid scolding = Stage 1 (pre-conventional, punishment). Child following rules to maintain social order = Stage 4.
- Carol Gilligan criticised Kohlberg for male bias ("ethic of care").

### 2.4 Erikson: Psychosocial Development (8 stages, "Eight Ages of Man")

| Stage | Age | Crisis | Virtue |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0-1 | **Trust vs Mistrust** | Hope |
| 2 | 1-3 | Autonomy vs Shame & Doubt | Will |
| 3 | 3-6 | Initiative vs Guilt | Purpose |
| 4 | 6-12 | **Industry vs Inferiority** (school age) | Competence |
| 5 | 12-18 | **Identity vs Role Confusion** (adolescence) | Fidelity |
| 6 | Young adult | Intimacy vs Isolation | Love |
| 7 | Middle adult | Generativity vs Stagnation | Care |
| 8 | Old age | Ego Integrity vs Despair | Wisdom |

- Adolescence is for Erikson a period of "identity crisis" (term by Erikson). A **moratorium** is a time of exploration before commitment (Marcia added identity statuses).
- Erikson is a neo-Freudian: stresses social factors (Freud: sexual).

### 2.5 Freud: Psychosexual Theory & Personality Structure

| Stage | Age | Erogenous zone / fixation |
|---|---|---|
| **Oral** | 0-1 | Mouth; thumb-sucking, smoking later |
| **Anal** | 1-3 | Toilet training; orderliness/messiness |
| **Phallic** | 3-6 | Genitals; **Oedipus** (boy) and **Electra** (girl) complex |
| **Latency** | 6-puberty | Sexual urges dormant; social/skill growth |
| **Genital** | puberty on | Mature sexual interest |

- **Id**: pleasure principle, instinctive, unconscious, present at birth. **Ego**: reality principle, rational mediator. **Superego**: morality principle, conscience and ideal.
- Levels of mind: conscious, preconscious, **unconscious** (iceberg model).
- Defence mechanisms are covered in Chapter 7.

### 2.6 Bruner: Cognitive Growth and Discovery

- Three modes of representation: **Enactive** (action; 0-1), **Iconic** (images), **Symbolic** (language, symbols; abstract).
- **Discovery learning**; **Spiral curriculum** (return to topics at increasing complexity); readiness can be taught: "any subject can be taught effectively in some intellectually honest form to any child at any stage".
- Scaffolding concept developed with Wood and Ross. Book: *The Process of Education* (1960). Influenced constructivism.

### 2.7 Maslow: Hierarchy of Needs (humanistic)

```
 5. Self-actualisation  (realising potential)         ← growth / being need
 4. Esteem              (respect, status, achievement)
 3. Love & belongingness (friends, family)
 2. Safety / security   (shelter, job, order)
 1. Physiological       (food, water, sleep, air)       ← base
```

- Lower needs must be reasonably met before higher ones. Needs 1-4 are **deficiency (D) needs**; self-actualisation is a **being (B) need**. Later versions add cognitive (knowing) and aesthetic needs between esteem and self-actualisation, and self-transcendence at the top.
- Educational use: a hungry or insecure child cannot learn; mid-day meal; safe, supportive classroom.
- Carl Rogers: unconditional positive regard; "fully functioning person"; learner-centred.

**⚡ Exam tip:** Memorise numbers: Piaget 4 stages (ages 0-2, 2-7, 7-11, 11+); Kohlberg 3 levels/6 stages; Erikson 8 stages (Trust vs Mistrust first, Identity vs Role Confusion at 12-18, Industry vs Inferiority 6-12); Freud 5 stages; Maslow 5 levels (base = physiological, top = self-actualisation). "Searching for a hidden toy" = object permanence; "ZPD" = Vygotsky; "scaffolding" = temporary support.

## 3. Theories of Learning

**Learning** = relatively permanent change in behaviour due to experience/practice (not maturation, fatigue, drugs).

### 3.1 Behaviourist: Classical Conditioning (Ivan Pavlov, Russian physiologist; dog experiment)

| Term | Meaning | Pavlov's example |
|---|---|---|
| **UCS** (Unconditioned Stimulus) | Naturally triggers response | Food |
| **UCR** (Unconditioned Response) | Natural, unlearned response | Salivation to food |
| **NS** (Neutral Stimulus) | Initially no response | Bell (before conditioning) |
| **CS** (Conditioned Stimulus) | Neutral stimulus after pairing | Bell |
| **CR** (Conditioned Response) | Learned response to CS | Salivation to bell alone |

- Sequence: before conditioning, bell (NS) → no salivation. Pairing: bell + food → salivation. After conditioning: bell alone → salivation.
- **Acquisition**: learning phase. **Extinction**: CS presented repeatedly without UCS and CR fades. **Spontaneous recovery**: extinguished CR reappears after rest. **Generalisation**: similar stimuli evoke CR. **Discrimination**: respond only to the specific CS. **Higher-order conditioning**. 
- John B. Watson: **Little Albert** experiment (fear of white rat) shows emotional conditioning; founder of behaviourism. Classical conditioning explains phobias, emotional attitudes toward school/teacher.
- Contiguity: stimuli close in time. Backward conditioning is least effective.

### 3.2 Operant Conditioning (B.F. Skinner; Skinner box; rats/pigeons)

- Behaviour is shaped by its **consequences**. Also called instrumental / Type-R conditioning (Pavlov's: Type-S).
- **Reinforcement** increases behaviour; **punishment** decreases behaviour.

| | Add stimulus | Remove stimulus |
|---|---|---|
| Increases behaviour | **Positive reinforcement** (praise, reward) | **Negative reinforcement** (relief, e.g., seatbelt alarm stops) |
| Decreases behaviour | **Positive punishment** (scolding) | **Negative punishment** (loss of privilege, response cost) |

- Negative reinforcement is **not** punishment.
- **Shaping**: reinforcing successive approximations. **Chaining**. **Programmed instruction/teaching machines** originate from Skinner (small steps, immediate feedback, self-paced, linear programme).
- **Schedules of reinforcement**

| Schedule | Rule | Response pattern |
|---|---|---|
| Continuous (CRF) | Every response | Fastest learning, fastest extinction |
| Fixed Ratio (FR) | After fixed number of responses (piece-work pay) | High rate, pause after reward |
| Variable Ratio (VR) | After random number (gambling, slot machine) | **Highest, steady rate; most resistant to extinction** |
| Fixed Interval (FI) | First response after fixed time (monthly salary; "scalloped" pattern) | Burst near the deadline |
| Variable Interval (VI) | After random time (pop quiz) | Moderate steady rate |

- Partial (intermittent) reinforcement resists extinction more than continuous.

### 3.3 Thorndike: Connectionism (trial and error; cat in puzzle box)

- Learning = forming **S-R bonds** (connections) between stimulus and response; **trial-and-error / selection and connection**.
- **Primary laws**:
  1. **Law of Readiness**: learning is effective when the learner is physically/mentally ready; satisfying when ready and allowed to act.
  2. **Law of Exercise**: *Use* strengthens connection (repetition), *disuse* weakens it. (Later Thorndike abandoned this.)
  3. **Law of Effect**: responses followed by satisfaction are strengthened; followed by annoyance are weakened. (Most important; the basis for reinforcement.)
- Secondary laws: multiple response, set/attitude, prepotency of elements, response by analogy, associative shifting.
- **Transfer**: *theory of identical elements*.

### 3.4 Cognitive: Gestalt/Insight Learning (Kohler, Koffka, Wertheimer)

- "Gestalt" = whole/pattern; "the whole is more than the sum of its parts". Learning by **insight** (sudden grasp of the relationship).
- **Wolfgang Kohler** studied chimpanzee **Sultan** (Tenerife): joined two sticks to reach a banana; used boxes. "Aha experience".
- Features of insight: sudden, based on perception of the whole, transfers easily, retained well.
- Laws of perception: proximity, similarity, closure, continuity, figure-ground. Teaching: present the whole first, then parts; problem-solving approach.

### 3.5 Bandura: Social (Observational) Learning (Social Cognitive Theory)

- Learning by **observing and imitating** models (**modelling**, vicarious learning). **Bobo doll** experiment (children imitated aggression).
- Four processes: **Attention → Retention → Reproduction (motor) → Motivation (reinforcement)**.
- **Vicarious reinforcement**: seeing a model rewarded raises imitation. **Self-efficacy**: belief in one's ability to succeed (central to motivation).
- Teacher's role as a model; role of media and peers.

### 3.6 Other Learning Theorists

| Theorist | Key idea |
|---|---|
| **Tolman** | Sign-gestalt/latent learning (rats in a maze build a "cognitive map") |
| **Hull** | Drive-reduction theory (need → drive → reinforcement) |
| **Gagné** | Hierarchy of learning (8 types: signal → S-R → chaining → verbal association → discrimination → concept → rule → problem solving); **9 events of instruction**: (1) gain attention (2) inform objectives (3) recall prior learning (4) present stimulus (5) provide guidance (6) elicit performance (7) give feedback (8) assess performance (9) enhance retention and transfer |
| **Ausubel** | **Meaningful reception learning**; **advance organizers** (introductory overview linking new to existing knowledge); subsumption; against rote learning; "the most important single factor is what the learner already knows" |
| **Bruner** | Discovery learning, spiral curriculum |
| **Constructivism** | Learners **actively construct** knowledge from experience and prior knowledge; teacher = facilitator; stresses collaborative learning, inquiry, problem-based learning; NCF 2005 and NEP 2020 are constructivist |
| **Rogers / Maslow** | Humanistic, experiential, learner-centred |
| **Lewin** | Field theory, B = f(P, E) |
| **Kolb** | Experiential learning cycle: concrete experience → reflective observation → abstract conceptualisation → active experimentation |

- 5E model (constructivist): Engage, Explore, Explain, Elaborate, Evaluate. (Project-based activities fall under Elaborate/Explore.)

### 3.7 Comparison of Conditioning

| Feature | Classical (Pavlov) | Operant (Skinner) |
|---|---|---|
| Learner | Passive | Active, operates on environment |
| Based on | Stimulus before response | Consequence after response |
| Response | Involuntary/reflex | Voluntary |
| Unit | S-R association | R-S (reinforcement) |

**⚡ Exam tip:** "Before conditioning, bell is a **neutral** stimulus; after pairing, **conditioned**; food is **UCS**; salivation to food is **UCR**." "Cat puzzle box → trial and error (Thorndike)"; "rat/pigeon, Skinner box → operant"; "chimpanzee Sultan → insight (Kohler)"; "Bobo doll → Bandura". Variable ratio = most resistant to extinction.
